Mouse Study Links Chronic Choline Restriction to Insulin Resistance

By Nutranexa News | August 18, 2026

UNC researchers report that long-term choline restriction in mice remodeled liver lipid metabolism and drove insulin resistance through a CD36-ETNPPL regulatory axis.

Study Design

Researchers at the UNC Nutrition Research Institute examined how long-term choline restriction affected metabolism in mice, including effects on liver lipid handling and glucose regulation.

The work, published in July 2026, focused on the hepatic pathways that connect dietary choline supply to systemic insulin sensitivity.

Main Findings

The study found that chronic choline restriction remodeled hepatic lipid metabolism and drove insulin resistance, with the effects traced to a CD36-ETNPPL regulatory axis.

According to the UNC report, the choline-restricted mice did not become obese, indicating the metabolic effects were not simply secondary to weight gain.

Buyer Takeaway

The findings add to a growing body of animal and epidemiological evidence framing choline as relevant to metabolic health, not only cognition.

For choline ingredient suppliers, the study supports interest in choline-enriched metabolic and healthy-aging nutrition formats, though human confirmation is still needed.
