Phosphatidylserine Italy Market Entry: An Importer's Guide to Notification, Labeling and Import

Place a phosphatidylserine food supplement on the Italian market — pre-market notification to the Ministry of Health, Italian-language labeling conventions.

Published November 19, 2026

View PS ProductsRequest Specifications

Italy is one of Europe's largest food supplement markets, and it regulates the category through a national layer on top of EU law. Customs are European — goods clear the EU's common external border like in any member state — but the supplement itself faces a distinctly Italian gate: a pre-market notification to the Ministry of Health, labeling that must work in Italian, and market surveillance by national and local health authorities. For a brand owner sourcing phosphatidylserine from outside the EU, the entry file is written twice — for Europe and for Italy — and the second version decides whether the product reaches shelf. This guide walks through that file in an importer's order: notification, label, novel food status, customs, supplier documents and the pitfalls in between. If you are still selecting the base material, review Nutranexa's soy-derived and sunflower-derived phosphatidylserine grades before fixing the specification the Italian file will hang from.

Why Italy Is a Distinct Market-Entry File Inside the EU

Buyers who have already cleared one EU market often assume an Italy-ready product comes free with an EU-ready one. The assumption breaks in three places. First, notification: Italy runs its own pre-market notification regime for food supplements, administered nationally rather than through any EU-level register. Second, language: EU Regulation 1169/2011 sets the mandatory food information every label must carry, but Italy's national implementation requires that information to be in Italian, and Italian inspectors read the label in Italian. Third, enforcement: national authorities and the local health units (ASL) covering each territory run market checks on supplements sold in pharmacies, parapharmacies and online. None of this replaces the EU rules — it stacks on top of them, and the additions surface only after the goods have shipped.

Pre-Market Notification to the Ministero della Salute

Italy requires every food supplement to be notified to the Ministero della Salute before it is placed on the Italian market. Submission is electronic, through the Ministry's online notification portal, and the record is held in the national database of notified supplements. The notification identifies the product — its name, its category as a food supplement and the business placing it on the market — and carries the label information that matters for oversight: ingredients and their content relative to the allotted daily dose. Two properties matter commercially. It is product-specific, so each SKU needs its own record. And it is a living file: changes to composition, name or label data flow through as updates to the same record. Buyer actions: decide early who files — in practice, the Italian-established business placing the product — and treat the notification data as a controlled document derived from the final approved label. For how notification regimes differ across European markets, see the European food supplement notification guide.

Italian-Language Labeling and the Allotted Daily Dose Conventions

The mandatory particulars set by EU Regulation 1169/2011 — product name, ingredient list with allergen emphasis, quantity, durability marking, storage conditions, the responsible food business operator's name and address, and the nutrition declaration — must appear in Italian on supplements sold in Italy. Beyond the mandatory particulars, Italian supplement labels follow well-recognized conventions: the designation "integratore alimentare", a statement of the recommended daily dose, and the standard warnings — do not exceed the stated daily dose, supplements are not a substitute for a varied and balanced diet, keep the product out of the reach of young children. Artwork review should include an Italian-native regulatory reader, not only a translator. The allergen emphasis deserves particular care when the raw material is soy-derived, because the allergen in the ingredient analysis must match what the label emphasizes. Language sits inside a wider multilingual problem — our guide to multilingual mandatory food information in Europe shows how buyers structure label versions across markets without letting translations drift.

Novel Food Status for Soy- and Sunflower-Derived PS

Whether phosphatidylserine may be sold as a supplement at all is decided at EU level, not nationally. Italy applies Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 on novel foods exactly as the other member states do — there is no separate Italian novel food approval, but Italian authorities do enforce the EU status, and a product whose source lacks proper standing is as unsaleable in Milan as anywhere else in the Union. Soy lecithin-derived PS has decades of commercial history and is the source most buyers can document without difficulty. Sunflower-derived PS, produced by enzymatic transphosphatidylation, is newer, and its status should be verified against the EU Novel Food Catalogue and the authorization list before any volume commitment, not after. Buyer action: obtain the source-specific regulatory basis from the supplier during the first sample cycle and file it beside the Italian notification so both records tell the same story.

The Import Path: EU Customs and the Italian-Registered Responsible Party

There is no separate Italian import licence for food supplements. Goods enter through EU customs under the common external tariff: the importer needs an EORI number, a customs declaration with the correct tariff classification, and duty and VAT treatment per EU-wide rules. What Italy adds is an expectation about who stands behind the product. The notification and the label both point to a business, and in practice that business is established in Italy — an Italian-registered importer, distributor or the brand's own local entity acting as the responsible party. Brand owners without an Italian presence route the file through an Italian-registered partner, and that choice is a compliance decision, not just a logistics one: the partner's name and address go on the label, their identity anchors the notification record, and the two must stay aligned for every batch. Select the partner before the artwork is finalized, because re-printing labels to swap a responsible party is a cost no launch budget anticipates.

Documentation to Demand from the Supplier

The Italian file is only as strong as the ingredient documents behind it, so demand the mapped set before the first bulk order. The lot-specific certificate of analysis with a named assay method anchors the dose claim the Italian notification repeats. The allergen statement matters doubly in Italy, because soy-derived PS triggers the allergen emphasis the Italian label must carry. The GMO status declaration supports EU GMO labeling rules, and an irradiation statement should confirm the material has not been treated — irradiated foodstuffs face EU authorization limits and mandatory labeling, and undeclared treatment is taken seriously in Italian checks. Round the pack out with residue and contaminant data against EU limits, and keep every document lot-linked so a batch checked at customs or by a local health unit can be traced end to end.

Practical Pitfalls: Notification–Label Mismatch and Batch Rescue

The most common Italian failure is a mismatch between the notification record and the printed label — a product name revised in artwork, a dose table edited in marketing, a new excipient that never reached the notification file. Italian reviewers read the two side by side, and discrepancies invite questions the brand cannot answer quickly from abroad. The second pitfall is importing a label that passed in another member state but lacks the Italian particulars or conventions, which surfaces only at market check. The third is batch rescue: where the defect is correctable, relabeling under controlled conditions before the goods reach the market is usually the commercial answer; where it is not, withdrawal or re-export beats release-and-hope every time. The buyer actions that prevent all three: a label change-control process tied to the notification record, a pre-shipment label review against the Italian checklist, and a written agreement with the Italian-registered partner about who acts when a batch needs fixing.

FAQ

Q: Does phosphatidylserine need novel food approval to sell in Italy?

A: Novel food status is determined at EU level under Regulation (EU) 2015/2283, and Italy applies it directly — there is no separate Italian approval. Soy lecithin-derived PS has long-established EU commercial history; sunflower-derived PS should be verified against the EU Novel Food Catalogue and authorization list for the specific source before committing volume.

Q: Who submits the supplement notification to the Ministry of Health?

A: The business under whose responsibility the product is placed on the Italian market — typically an Italian-registered importer, distributor or the brand's local entity. The notification is product-specific and must be updated when composition, name or label data change.

Q: Can we sell with an English label plus an Italian leaflet?

A: No. The mandatory food information required by EU Regulation 1169/2011 must appear in Italian on the label itself under Italy's national implementation, together with the Italian conventions such as the recommended daily dose statement and the standard warnings. A leaflet does not substitute for compliant on-pack information.

Q: What should an importer hold ready for a customs or ASL check?

A: The notification record, the final approved label proof, lot-specific COAs with named assay methods, allergen, GMO and irradiation statements, and residue data against EU limits — all traceable to the batch. This pack lets the importer answer a check on the spot.

Conclusion

Italy rewards importers who treat it as a national layer rather than a shipping destination: the Ministero della Salute notification, the Italian-language label and the Italian-registered responsible party shape everything else, and all three depend on the ingredient documentation you demand before the first bulk order. Fix the label and the notification together, verify novel food status by source, and give every batch a paper trail that survives a customs or market check.
