Phosphatidylserine China Market Entry: An Importer's Guide to Health Food Registration, Labeling and Import

Bring a phosphatidylserine supplement into China — SAMR health food registration vs filing, the cross-border e-commerce channel, Chinese labeling standards.

Published November 20, 2026

View PS ProductsRequest Specifications

China is one of the largest consumer markets for brain-health and active-aging supplements, and the most structurally different market a phosphatidylserine brand can enter. The gate is not a single notification: general trade requires a national health food registration, the cross-border e-commerce channel runs on a separate policy track, labels must work in Chinese under national standards, and the overseas factory itself needs registration with China Customs before its goods can clear. The entry route chosen at the planning stage decides the regulatory budget, the timeline and the sales channels available afterwards. This guide walks through that decision in an importer's order: registration, the cross-border alternative, labeling, customs and supplier documentation. If the raw-material specification is not yet fixed, review Nutranexa's soy-derived phosphatidylserine grades first — the source and grade you select will anchor every document in the Chinese file.

Why China Is a Distinct Market-Entry File

Buyers who have cleared Japan or Korea often expect China to be one more APAC file with different forms. The difference is deeper. First, structure: China separates supplements into health foods (保健食品), which need national registration or filing before general-trade sale, and ordinary foods, which cannot carry health function claims. Second, channels: a product sold through cross-border e-commerce lives under a different policy framework from the same product sold through a domestic distributor. Third, enforcement: customs inspection at the border, platform checks online and market surveillance offline each read the file differently. A programme that does not decide its channel first will usually discover the mismatch only after stock has shipped.

Health Food Registration vs Filing Under SAMR

The health food system is administered nationally by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) through its registration and filing procedures. The filing route is fast but narrow: it applies to raw materials on the official raw-material catalogue, a limited set of vitamins, minerals and other listed ingredients. Registration is the broader route: product testing, formula assessment and, for function claims, evaluation against the approved claim framework, ending with an approval certificate specific to the formula and claim. Phosphatidylserine holds new-food-ingredient status in China, but the approved scope of that status — source, specification and permitted use — must be confirmed against the current official announcement before any general-trade use is assumed. Buyer actions: decide early whether the programme is a health food with a functional claim or an ordinary food without one, and verify the PS announcement scope during the first sample cycle.

The Cross-Border E-Commerce Channel

China's cross-border e-commerce (CBEC) retail import policy created a second door. Products on the CBEC retail import positive list can be sold to Chinese consumers through CBEC platforms without prior health food registration, on the basis that they are consumer-ordered personal-use purchases. The channel carries its own constraints: per-order and annual per-person purchase limits, digital transaction records flowing to customs, and a hard rule that CBEC goods are sold through the cross-border channel — not re-labeled into offline retail. For a phosphatidylserine brand, CBEC is how most new foreign supplements reach Chinese consumers first: the regulatory lead time is a fraction of a registration, and platform scrutiny rewards brands that can produce a clean document set on request.

Chinese-Language Labeling and GB Standards

General-trade imported food must carry a Chinese label conforming to the national prepackaged food labeling standard (GB 7718) and the nutrition labeling standard (GB 28050): product name, ingredient list with allergen declaration, net content, dates and storage conditions, and the name and address of the Chinese importer or agent — in Chinese, on the pack. A health food adds the approval number and the health food mark, claims limited to what the registration approves, and the recommended intake statement. Label review for soy-derived phosphatidylserine should check the allergen declaration against the raw material's allergen status, because the ingredient analysis and the printed declaration must tell the same story. CBEC goods are not exempt from consumer-facing information: platform listings and inserts still need accurate Chinese content, so artwork sign-off should include a Chinese-native regulatory reader on both routes.

The Import Path: Customs and Overseas Manufacturer Registration

China Customs requires overseas manufacturers of many food categories, including food ingredients, to be registered with the General Administration of Customs (GACC) before their products can be exported to China. For a phosphatidylserine supplier, that registration is a prerequisite, and buyers should verify the factory's GACC registration status and scope during supplier qualification — a step that sits naturally inside a broader review of the supplier's production license and scope documents. On the import side, the Chinese importer of record completes consignee filing, classifies the goods under the correct HS code, and faces customs declaration and inspection. A health food at general trade import is checked against its registration certificate, so certificate, label and goods must align at the border. A certificate of free sale from the origin market belongs in the same pack — our guide to the certificate of free sale for PS exports explains what that document proves.

Documentation to Demand from the Supplier

The Chinese file is assembled from ingredient documents the buyer should demand before the first bulk order. The lot-specific certificate of analysis with a named assay method anchors the product's actual content against any label or listing claim, and the specification document defines the grade and concentration so the imported goods match what the file describes. Documentation of the new-food-ingredient announcement and its scope supports the ingredient's regulatory basis, and the supplier's GACC registration details confirm the factory can legally ship into China at all. Add GMO, allergen and irradiation statements consistent with the origin-market pack, keep every document lot-linked so a customs or platform query can be answered batch by batch, and one core file then serves both routes: registration evidence for general trade, listing evidence for CBEC.

Practical Pitfalls: Channel–Claim Mismatch and Label Drift

The most common China failure is a channel–claim mismatch: goods bought for the cross-border channel end up offered through domestic retail, or a CBEC listing carries function wording that only a registered health food may use. Both are enforcement exposures, both preventable with a written channel policy inside the distribution agreement. The second pitfall is label drift — an artwork revision that breaks alignment between the Chinese label, the registration certificate or the platform listing, discovered only at customs or during a platform check. The third is assuming the ingredient status transfers: the PS announcement's scope, not the supplier's brochure, defines what can be claimed in China. Fix the channel before the purchase order, run label change control through one owner, and obtain the ingredient-status documentation during sample evaluation.

FAQ

Q: Does phosphatidylserine need health food registration to sell in China?

A: For general-trade sale as a health food with a function claim, yes — a SAMR registration specific to the product formula and claim is required. The filing route applies only to raw materials on the official catalogue, which should be checked for the current status of phosphatidylserine. Cross-border e-commerce sales of listed goods do not require prior registration, subject to the channel's limits — and CBEC goods cannot move into general-trade retail later without registration.

Q: What does GACC overseas manufacturer registration mean for our PS supplier?

A: Overseas factories exporting food and food ingredients to China must be registered with China Customs under the framework effective since 2022. Verify the supplier's registration status and scope during qualification — without it, the goods cannot lawfully enter Chinese general trade.

Q: What documents should an importer hold ready for a customs or platform check?

A: Lot-specific COAs with named assay methods, the grade specification, ingredient-status documentation for the PS announcement, the supplier's GACC registration details, GMO, allergen and irradiation statements, and label or listing proofs aligned to the sales channel — all traceable to the batch.

Conclusion

China rewards importers who choose the channel before anything else: registration versus cross-border e-commerce decides the timeline, the claims available and the way the goods can be sold, and every downstream document — label, listing, customs file — hangs from that decision. Verify the PS ingredient-status scope, confirm the factory's GACC registration, and hold a lot-linked document set that survives a border check. To build that set around the grade your China programme will use, work with Nutranexa's quality and R&D team on a market-fit specification — soy-derived and sunflower-derived grades supported with the COAs and declarations each route requires.
