Phosphatidylserine SFCR Import Licence Review for Canadian Buyers

Review whether your phosphatidylserine imports need a Safe Food for Canadians licence, who must hold it, and how to verify licence scope before shipping to.

출판 August 6, 2026

PS 제품 보기규격서 요청

When a Canadian buyer orders bulk 포스파티딜세린 (PS) from an overseas manufacturer, the first border question is not about the powder. It is about the licence the import transaction must carry. The direct answer: most food importers into Canada need a Safe Food for Canadians (SFC) licence issued by the Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA), issued for the activity "Importing Food" and the commodity being imported, and held by the Canadian importer of record — a foreign PS factory cannot be the licensed importer in most cases. CFIA verifies the licence number on the electronic import declaration, and a shipment without a valid number is denied entry until corrected.

This article is written for Canadian importers, distributors, supplement manufacturers, functional food brands, and their procurement and quality teams. It explains when an SFC licence applies to PS, who holds it, what it covers, and how the supplier's documents support the licensed importer's duties. Confirm your product, route, and transaction with CFIA and qualified counsel.

Why the Import Licence Is a Buyer-Side Question

Many first-time PS buyers assume licensing is a supplier responsibility, like an export certificate or a COA. In Canada it is the opposite: the Safe Food for Canadians Act and Regulations (SFCR) require that food imported into Canada be brought in by a person holding an SFC licence for importing food. The factory supplies documents and goods; the Canadian importer of record carries the licence.

Under section 26(1) of the SFCR (SOR/2018-108), any food is a prescribed food commodity for a licence to import, so the requirement is broad and bulk PS for food use sits inside it.

This changes how a buyer runs supplier qualification. Instead of asking only "what documents do you send," the Canadian buyer should ask "what will my licensed import operation need from you so the declaration and my preventive control plan hold together." The supplier's export documentation — invoice, packing list, bill of lading, batch COA, current specification, source and allergen statements, and wood packaging records — becomes the evidence base for the importer's file, as covered in the export documentation and ISPM-15 review.

Who Must Hold the SFC Licence

The licence holder is the person who imports the food, not the person who makes it: typically a Canadian supplement or food company, a distributor, a contract manufacturer importing on a customer's behalf, or — in narrow cases — a CFIA-recognized non-resident importer.

The importer of record with a fixed place of business

Under section 29(1) of the SFCR, the applicant must carry on business related to the food from a fixed place of business in Canada or a recognized foreign state. For almost every commercial PS transaction, the Canadian entity on the customs entry holds the licence — not the overseas seller.

Non-resident importers: the narrow exception

CFIA does allow a non-resident importer (NRI) to hold an SFC licence, but only under limited conditions. The NRI's fixed place of business must be in a country CFIA recognizes as having a food safety system at least equivalent to the SFCR. For food other than meat and live or raw shellfish, the United States is currently the only recognized foreign state for direct NRI shipments — and that arrangement excludes dietary supplements and natural health products.

The consequence: a Chinese manufacturer such as Nutranexa's parent operation does not qualify as the licensed importer into Canada, and a U.S.-based trader generally cannot use the NRI lane for supplement-type material either. The shipment must come in through a licensed Canadian importer of record.

What the SFC Licence Actually Covers

An SFC import licence is not a generic "food licence"; it is issued for specific activities and commodities that must match the transaction.

The activity: "Importing Food"

The licence must be issued for the declared international activity "Importing Food." A licence for manufacturing, processing, packaging, or labelling does not authorize importing — verify the declared activities in My CFIA first.

The commodity and sub-commodity scope

The application asks the applicant to identify food products from commodity and sub-commodity categories set out in Annex A of CFIA's guidance *What to consider before applying for an SFC licence*; these are generally broader than the definitions inside the SFCR. Confirm the PS powder fits the commodity on the licence, and amend the licence — free of charge through My CFIA — if a new product line does not fit.

HS code and import declaration alignment

The licence number is only part of the declaration. CFIA also requires the importer or broker to declare the appropriate Harmonized System (HS) code and Other Government Department (OGD) extension; the Automated Import Reference System (AIRS) is CFIA's reference tool for correct coding. This connects to the HS-code review for US and Canada importers.

Preventive Controls: The Duty That Comes Before the Licence

CFIA is explicit that an importer must prepare and implement a preventive control plan (PCP) before requesting an SFC licence: a written plan showing how the importer ensures imported food is safe and compliant with Canadian consumer protection requirements. For an ingredient importer, it typically covers supplier approval, specification review, COA verification, allergen control, recall procedures, and per-lot evidence.

There is a small-business exception: under sections 86 and 87 of the SFCR, a business whose gross annual sales from all food are $100,000 CAD or less is generally not required to have a written PCP, but it must still put preventive controls in place. Buyers above that threshold should treat the PCP as part of the licence application.

The supplier file matters here. The COA and specification review, 공급업체 온보딩, 그리고 peroxide value review are evidence a licensed importer can fold into a PCP: approved specifications, named test methods, and batch results.

Traceability: One Step Back, One Step Forward

Alongside licensing and preventive controls, the SFCR requires traceability for most food businesses that import: the ability to track a food one step back and one step forward, with records that include the lot or other unique identifier, the date, and the supplier and customer paths. The supplier's batch COA and lot number must survive into the importer's records, and downstream use of that lot must be documented.

At the Border: The Integrated Import Declaration and Licence Verification

CFIA verifies licences automatically through the Integrated Import Declaration (IID). On February 12, 2024, CFIA activated automatic verification of SFC licences for imports of manufactured foods, and transactions are automatically rejected unless a valid SFC licence is declared. Bulk PS imported for food use falls in this lane.

How the licence number must be declared

CFIA requires the licence number in the "Registration Number" field of IID exactly as issued, in an 8-character alphanumeric format such as A12B3CD4. Common rejects include:

The enforcement consequence is not a gentle warning: transactions without a valid licence are automatically rejected, the shipment is denied entry until the error is corrected and the declaration re-submitted, and non-compliance can lead to administrative monetary penalties or prosecution.

Licence Lifecycle: Validity, Renewal, and Timing

Two timing facts change procurement plans:

  • Two-year validity. Under section 33(1) of the SFCR, a licence expires two years after issuance or renewal. Add licence expiry to the annual supplier-review calendar so a PS plan never meets an expired number at the border.
  • Application and amendment time. The published service standard for issuing or amending an SFC licence is 70 business days, and complex files can take longer. New licences are subject to fees; amendments are free. For a first PS order, the licence should be in motion well before the PO, not after the container sails.

Renewal is not automatic: the licence holder applies through My CFIA, and the same preventive-control and traceability conditions apply. Buyers who treat the licence as permanent, or as something the supplier holds, discover the gap at the border.

How This Fits the NHP Route

Canada regulates PS-containing products through more than one route, and the licence depends on the route chosen. The Canada NHP vs supplemented food resource explains route selection; the licensing summary is:

  • If PS is imported as a food or supplemented-food ingredient, the importer needs the SFCR/SFC import licence described here.
  • If PS is imported as a natural health product (NHP) ingredient for an NHP, Health Canada's Natural Health Products Regulations require the importer to hold an NHP site licence, and the finished product generally needs a product licence with a Natural Product Number before it is sold. That is a different licence from the CFIA food-import licence.
  • A buyer planning to sell the same PS in both lanes needs both files coordinated, because the two systems verify different things.

This distinction matters before the PO. A supplier can confirm source, specification, and COA, but it cannot decide which licence lane applies to the buyer's finished product. That decision belongs to the buyer's regulatory and legal team, supported by the supplemented-food caution-box review when the food lane is chosen.

A Practical Pre-Order Checklist for Canadian PS Buyers

  1. Confirm the product route (food, supplemented food, or NHP) before licensing work starts.
  2. Name the importer of record in the PO and confirm that entity holds — or will apply for — the SFC import licence.
  3. Verify the licence covers "Importing Food" and the right commodity in My CFIA.
  4. Confirm the HS code and OGD extension for PS in AIRS, aligned with the customs broker.
  5. Prepare the written preventive control plan before the licence application, unless the small-business exception applies.
  6. Collect the supplier's current specification, batch COA, source and allergen statements, and export document pack.
  7. Add the licence expiry date and renewal lead time to the supplier-review calendar.
  8. Test the declaration path with the broker on a first small order before the first bulk arrival.
  9. Keep traceability records one step back and one step forward for each lot.
  10. Confirm in writing with CFIA or qualified counsel if the product, route, or trade structure is unusual.

Steps 2, 3, and 4 are contract and filing work. A supplier can support them with documents, but the Canadian buyer owns them. A PS purchase from a manufacturer that ships 25 kg drums with clear COAs and source files — like the routes described on the product pages — is much easier to bring through a licensed import operation than one with vague documents and no lot trail.

결론

The SFCR import licence is the Canadian buyer's border credential, and it cannot be outsourced to the factory. Before the first PS shipment reaches Canada, the importer of record should hold an SFC licence issued for "Importing Food" and the right commodity, have a preventive control plan and traceability records in place, and declare the licence number correctly on the Integrated Import Declaration. The supplier's documents turn that obligation into a controlled file.

For Canadian procurement and quality teams, the sequence is simple: confirm the route, name the licensed importer, verify licence scope, align the HS code and declaration data, and keep the supplier's lot-level evidence inside the PCP and traceability records. Do that before the PO, and the border step becomes a routine verification instead of a denied entry.

자주 묻는 질문

Do I need a Safe Food for Canadians licence to import phosphatidylserine into Canada?

In most cases, yes. Any food is a prescribed commodity for an import licence, and CFIA requires importers of most foods to hold an SFC licence for importing. Bulk PS for food use sits inside that requirement; confirm your product and route with CFIA.

Who must hold the SFC licence for a PS shipment?

The importer of record must hold it. Licence conditions generally require the applicant to carry on business from a fixed place of business in Canada or a recognized foreign state, so a foreign PS manufacturer cannot be the licensed importer in the standard case.

Can a Chinese PS manufacturer hold a Canadian food import licence?

Not in the normal situation. CFIA's non-resident importer lane is limited to countries with recognized food safety systems; for most foods the United States is currently the only recognized foreign state, with dietary supplements and natural health products excluded from that lane. A licensed Canadian importer must bring the goods in.

How do I verify that my SFC licence covers phosphatidylserine?

Log into My CFIA, open the issued permission, and confirm the declared activity is "Importing Food" and the commodity covers the PS product. Also confirm the HS code and OGD extension in AIRS match the declaration, because a valid licence for the wrong commodity can still reject the transaction.

What happens if a PS shipment arrives without a valid SFC licence number?

CFIA automatically verifies SFC licences through the Integrated Import Declaration. If the number is missing, wrong, expired, or outside the licence's scope, the transaction is rejected and the shipment is denied entry until the error is corrected and the declaration re-submitted. Non-compliance can also lead to enforcement action.

Is an SFCR import licence the same as a Health Canada NHP site licence?

No. The SFCR/SFC licence is CFIA's food-import licence for the food or supplemented-food route; the NHP site licence is Health Canada's licence for importers of natural health products. The route selected for the finished product decides which applies.
