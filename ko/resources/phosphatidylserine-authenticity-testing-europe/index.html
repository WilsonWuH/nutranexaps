Phosphatidylserine Authenticity Testing and Food-Fraud Review: A European Buyer Guide

How European buyers verify phosphatidylserine authenticity: source identity, lot-linked COA evidence, independent testing, and food-fraud controls.

출판 August 12, 2026

PS 제품 보기규격서 요청

Phosphatidylserine (PS) is sold by source and assay, so authenticity is a core buying question. The direct answer for a European buyer is to treat authenticity as its own verification file: confirm the exact source route and grade in writing, tie the specification and certificate of analysis (COA) to the exact lot shipped, check the identity and assay signals on that COA, keep the traceability records that connect raw material to dispatch, and decide in advance which lots justify independent verification. No single document proves authenticity by itself. A COA is evidence that must be checked for lot linkage, method, and consistency; a supplier statement is a claim to verify, not a fact to accept.

This guide explains why EU food law treats adulteration and fraudulent practices as compliance risks, what adulteration can look like for PS, what an authenticity evidence file should contain, which COA signals matter, when independent testing is justified, and which questions to ask before ordering. It is operational guidance for procurement, formulation, and quality teams, not legal or laboratory advice.

Why Authenticity Review Is Its Own Buyer Task

EU food law treats adulteration and fraud as compliance issues

European food law is explicit about adulteration. Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 states that food law shall aim to protect consumers and provide a basis for informed choices, and that it shall aim at the prevention of fraudulent or deceptive practices, the adulteration of food, and any other practices which may mislead the consumer. That principle applies to every operator in the chain, including an importer placing PS on the European market.

The enforcement layer is equally explicit. Regulation (EU) 2017/625, the Official Controls Regulation, brings fraudulent and deceptive practices within the scope of official controls performed by Member State authorities. For cross-border cases, the Commission coordinates the EU Agri-Food Fraud Network (FFN), which links the Commission with the liaison bodies designated by Member States. The IMSOC rules (Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/1715) define a food fraud notification as a non-compliance notification concerning any suspected intentional action by businesses or individuals for the purpose of deceiving purchasers and gaining undue advantage. In plain terms: fraud is intentional deception for advantage, and adulteration is one of the practices EU food law aims to prevent.

Authenticity is separate from contaminant testing and COA arithmetic

Most PS quality work asks whether a batch meets its specification: assay, moisture, heavy metals, microbiology, and other parameters. Authenticity asks a different question: is the powder actually PS of the declared source and grade, and does the paperwork belong to this batch? A batch can meet every contaminant limit and still be mislabeled, diluted, or substituted. So authenticity review sits alongside, not inside, routine specification review. The COA and specification review guide explains how to verify that a certificate is complete and consistent; this guide adds the identity and provenance layer on top of it.

What Adulteration Could Look Like for Phosphatidylserine

Adulteration does not need to be dramatic to be costly. For a purified phospholipid ingredient like PS, the realistic patterns are dilution, substitution, mislabeling, and document manipulation.

Assay dilution or mis-formulation

PS is sold in commercial assay bands, with Nutranexa offering 20%, 50%, and 70% PS grades as standard route options. If a powder is diluted with cheaper filler while still being invoiced as a 50% grade, the batch is adulterated by mis-formulation. The first signal is usually the assay: a result below the declared band, or a result that only barely meets the minimum while the grade implies a higher nominal content. Assay testing on the exact lot, checked against the quoted grade, catches this pattern.

Phospholipid substitution

A more sophisticated pattern is partial substitution: replacing some of the PS with a cheaper phospholipid or lecithin-derived material while reporting a PS content that still fits the specification band. The assay alone may look acceptable because it measures PS content, not the total phospholipid profile. Clues appear when the buyer compares the phospholipid context over time or against a reference sample. This is why an identity test plus a consistent phospholipid profile is more informative than an assay number alone, and why the same grade should look similar across lots from the same source route.

Source mislabeling: soy vs sunflower

Nutranexa maintains separate 두유 PS 그리고 해바라기 PS routes because source affects 알레르기 유발 진술, GMO-related statements, and brand positioning. If a supplier ships soy-derived PS while the paperwork claims sunflower-derived PS, or vice versa, the finished product label can be wrong for the European market. Source authenticity is verified with documented raw-material control and process traceability, not with a powder photograph.

Document-level manipulation

The highest-impact fraud in ingredient supply is often document fraud: a COA that belongs to a different lot, a reused certificate, an expired specification presented as current, or a test report never performed for the shipped batch. FFN reporting categories include record tampering and document fraud. This pattern is why lot linkage is the backbone of the authenticity file: the COA number, lot number, packaging date, invoice reference, and packing list should all point to the same physical batch.

Reading a COA for Authenticity Signals

The COA is the most common authenticity document a buyer receives; read it for identity, not only numbers. The COA 리뷰 가이드 covers the full verification workflow; the authenticity-specific signals are these:

  • Lot linkage. The COA should carry a lot or batch number that also appears on the drum labels, packing list, and commercial invoice. A COA without a lot reference, or with a lot number that matches nothing else in the shipment, cannot authenticate anything.
  • Identity line. A useful COA reports an identification test or identity result, not only assay, moisture, and contaminants. Identity testing is what distinguishes "this is PS" from "this powder is within the assay band".
  • Method references. The COA should name or reference the test methods used; identity and assay results are only interpretable if the method is known.
  • Assay band consistency. The result should fit the ordered grade and should be consistent with previous lots of the same route. Large unexplained swings between lots are a reason to question the certificate or the batch.
  • Issue date and authorization. The COA should be current, dated, and authorized for the shipment. An old or unsigned certificate is a document-control problem, and document-control problems are authenticity risks.
  • What the COA does not prove. A COA does not prove provenance, allergen status, or freedom from substitution. Those are answered by source declarations, traceability records, and independent verification where justified.

Independent Verification: When and What to Test

Independent testing is one layer of authenticity assurance, not the whole file. The third-party testing guide explains how to design an outside-lab program; for authenticity specifically:

  • Qualification testing on the first commercial lot of a new supplier or route, with identity and assay on the same sample plus the phospholipid context the lab can report.
  • Monitoring testing on an agreed frequency for repeat orders, weighted to risk: new source route, new factory line, or a price that looks out of line with the market.
  • Dispute testing when a COA signal is inconsistent or a shipment fails incoming checks, using a retained sample drawn and sealed at receipt.

For every outside test, agree the sample identity, methods, and reporting basis before the sample leaves your warehouse, and compare the independent result with the supplier COA for the same lot. An unexplained difference between the two is an authenticity finding, not a rounding issue. Record the independent report in the lot file for the next repeat-order document review.

Supplier Questionnaire on Authenticity

Ask these questions during 공급업체 온보딩 and before first bulk orders; each surfaces a document or a control rather than a reassurance:

  1. What is the exact source route for this grade, and will you state it in writing on the specification (soy PS, sunflower PS, or other)?
  2. Which identity and assay methods support the specification, and can you name or reference them?
  3. Can every COA be tied to the exact lot shipped, with the same lot number on the COA, drum labels, packing list, and invoice?
  4. What raw-material and process records connect the source material to the finished PS lot?
  5. How do you prevent soy and sunflower production from mixing, and what records prove it?
  6. Are retain samples kept for finished PS lots, and for how long?
  7. What change control applies if the source, process, or specification changes? (The supplier quality agreement should capture this in writing.)
  8. Will you support independent verification on a commercial lot if the buyer requests it?

A supplier who can answer all eight with documents is easier to qualify than one who answers with reassurance. The food-safety certificate review 그리고 manufacturer audit workflows add the facility-level controls behind those answers.

Red Flags and Escalation

Some warning signs deserve a hold decision before money moves:

  • A price far below the market range for the declared grade and source.
  • A COA with no lot number, no identity line, or no method references.
  • Source descriptions that stay vague ("plant-derived PS") when asked to confirm soy or sunflower.
  • Refusal to provide source-specific statements or the traceability records behind them.
  • Assay or phospholipid results that swing sharply between lots of the same grade.
  • Pressure to release a lot before independent verification completes.
  • A certificate that does not match the current specification or the shipment references.

If a red flag appears, hold the lot, keep samples sealed, request the missing evidence in writing, and verify before release. If an intentional fraudulent practice is suspected, keep the file and contact the competent national food authority; national authorities coordinate cross-border cases through the EU Food Fraud Network.

자주 묻는 질문

How can European buyers tell if phosphatidylserine is adulterated?

No single test catches every pattern. Build a layered file: a specific written source declaration, a lot-linked specification and COA with an identity line and methods, consistent assay and phospholipid context across lots, traceability from raw material to dispatch, and independent verification on qualification, monitoring, or dispute lots.

Does a COA prove that a phosphatidylserine batch is authentic?

No. A COA is evidence that must itself be verified: the lot number should match the drums and shipment documents, results should fit the declared grade, methods should be referenced, and the certificate should be current and authorized. It does not prove provenance or rule out substitution.

What does EU food law require around adulteration and food fraud?

Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 says food law shall aim to prevent fraudulent or deceptive practices, the adulteration of food, and misleading practices. Regulation (EU) 2017/625 brings fraudulent and deceptive practices within official controls, and the EU Agri-Food Fraud Network coordinates cross-border cases. For a buyer, the requirement is due diligence: the ingredient and its paperwork must match what is placed on the market.

How do buyers verify the soy vs sunflower source of phosphatidylserine?

Source is verified with documents and controls: a written source declaration, raw-material and process traceability, evidence that soy and sunflower production are kept separate, and source-related allergen and GMO statements. Where the claim is commercially important, independent verification on a commercial lot is a reasonable extra step.

What should a buyer do if a PS lot fails an authenticity check?

Hold the lot, keep samples sealed, request the missing evidence in writing, and run an independent retest before release. Record the finding in the lot file and escalate through the supplier quality agreement. If fraud is suspected, keep the evidence and contact the competent national food authority.

결론

Authenticity is the question every other quality document assumes away: that the powder is PS, that it is the declared source and grade, and that the certificate belongs to the lot. For a European buyer of bulk PS, the practical answer is a controlled evidence file reviewed before the order and before each release: specific source declaration, lot-linked specification and COA, identity and assay signals checked for consistency, traceability kept one step back and one step forward, and independent verification used deliberately where risk justifies it. EU food law already treats adulteration and fraud as compliance risks; a verification file turns that legal baseline into a repeatable procurement habit.
