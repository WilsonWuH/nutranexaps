Phosphatidylserine Supplier Quality Agreement: A US-EU Buyer Review

Review the clauses that matter in a phosphatidylserine supplier quality agreement: specification, COA, change control, audit rights, traceability, and claims.

출판 August 4, 2026

PS 제품 보기규격서 요청

A supplier quality agreement is the document where buyer and supplier agree on what the ingredient must be, how each side proves it, and what happens when something changes or goes wrong. For bulk phosphatidylserine (PS), the practical answer is short: draft or review the quality agreement before the first purchase order, attach the current specification and COA evidence to it, and build the agreement around the same documents and workflows your quality team already uses for incoming inspection. An agreement that names the product and supplier but never defines assay limits, test methods, change notification, traceability records, or audit rights protects no one when a batch question appears.

This article is written for importers, distributors, supplement manufacturers, functional food brands, procurement managers, quality teams, and regulatory reviewers in Europe and North America. It focuses on the buyer's side of a PS supplier quality agreement: the clauses worth reviewing, the US and EU rules that make those clauses useful, and the practical checklist to run before signing or renewing.

Why the Quality Agreement Is a Separate Buyer Task

PS supplier qualification usually involves several document layers: COA and specification review, supplier onboarding and document control, third-party testing, certificates such as food-safety certification review, 그리고 customer questionnaires. A quality agreement is a different layer: it converts those document checks into continuing obligations that both parties can enforce and audit.

Three distinctions matter:

  1. A COA proves something about one batch. A quality agreement defines how every batch will be documented for the life of the supply relationship.
  2. A certificate or questionnaire describes what a supplier claims to do. A quality agreement says what the buyer expects, what the supplier commits to, and what happens on a deviation.
  3. A purchase order covers price, quantity, and delivery terms. A quality agreement covers the quality interface that sits behind those orders, including change control and record retention.

Buyers who skip this step usually discover the gap in one of three moments: when a new batch arrives with a different carrier or specification, when the supplier changes a process or source without notice, or when an out-of-specification result needs a corrective-action trail. The quality agreement is the document that makes those moments manageable.

What US and EU Rules Expect From the Buyer Side

Neither the United States nor the EU requires a specific "supplier quality agreement" form. Instead, both regulatory systems create obligations that a well-written agreement supports. The clauses below should be read as practical ways to satisfy those obligations, not as legal advice.

US dietary supplements: 21 CFR Part 111

For dietary supplement manufacturers, 21 CFR 111.75 requires identity verification of dietary ingredients before use. For other components, a manufacturer may rely on a supplier's certificate of analysis only under defined conditions: the supplier must first be qualified by confirming the reliability of its COA results, the COA must include a description of the test or examination methods used, the limits of those tests, and the actual results, the manufacturer must document how the supplier was qualified, must periodically re-confirm the supplier's COA, and quality control personnel must review and approve the qualification documentation.

Section 111.95 then requires records including the specifications established and the documentation of supplier qualification for the purpose of relying on the supplier's COA. A quality agreement that names the specification, the COA content, the qualification basis, and the re-confirmation frequency is a natural home for those records.

US food: 21 CFR Part 117 Subpart G

When PS is used as an ingredient in food covered by the preventive controls rule, the receiving facility must establish a written, risk-based supply-chain program with approved suppliers, appropriate supplier verification activities, and documented verification (21 CFR 117.405 through 117.430). Verification activities can include onsite audits, sampling and testing, and review of the supplier's relevant food safety records. Where a hazard controlled by the supplier could result in serious adverse health consequences, an onsite audit is generally required before use and at least annually.

For PS buyers this means the quality agreement can serve as the written interface that identifies which verification activities apply, who conducts them, and how the results are documented.

EU: traceability and hygiene baselines

In the EU, Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 establishes general traceability obligations: food business operators must be able to identify any person from whom they received a food or substance intended to be incorporated into food, and the businesses to which they have supplied products. Article 18 is therefore a one-step-back, one-step-forward record-keeping obligation that a PS quality agreement can operationalize by naming lot records, batch COA references, and shipment traceability data.

Regulation (EC) No 852/2004 requires food business operators to put in place, implement, and maintain permanent procedures based on the HACCP principles, including hazard analysis, critical control points, monitoring, corrective actions, verification, and documentation. A European buyer's own system must explain how supplier controls fit the finished-product hazard analysis. The quality agreement is the practical document that connects supplier data, COA evidence, and deviation records to that system.

The same logic applies on the US side to FSVP importer responsibilities 그리고 incoming inspection and warehouse release: the agreement should not replace those workflows, but it should reference them so each side knows who produces which record.

The Core Clauses to Review

1. Scope, product identity, and source

The scope clause should name the exact product code, source route (soy or sunflower), target PS assay, and manufacturing site. For PS, source matters because soy and sunflower routes carry different label, allergen, and documentation implications. A scope that says only "phosphatidylserine" leaves room for later disagreement about which source, grade, or packaging was approved.

2. Specification and test-method attachment

The agreement should attach or reference the current controlled specification as an exhibit, including the test basis and method references. When Nutranexa provides product documents, buyers should confirm the specification version and compare it with the sample COA highlights shown on the public site, such as the PS 20% sunflower and PS 50% examples. A specification is only useful when both sides are testing against the same version.

3. COA obligations and COA reliance

This clause should state what each batch COA must contain: product name and code, batch or lot number, manufacture and report dates, test method description, specification limits, and actual results. It should also state when the COA will be provided relative to shipment and whether the buyer may rely on the COA for incoming release. Under 21 CFR 111.75, US supplement manufacturers may rely on a supplier COA only after qualifying the supplier and documenting that qualification; the agreement can make the qualification evidence explicit.

4. Change notification and deviation control

Change notification is often the most commercially important clause in a PS quality agreement. The supplier should commit to notifying the buyer in advance of any change that could affect the ingredient: source or raw material change, process or equipment change, specification revision, packaging change, or site change. The clause should define the notice window, the approval process, and what happens if no response is given. Deviation control should state how out-of-specification results are reported, how corrective actions are documented, and how repeat-order change control records are kept.

5. Traceability, lot records, and retained samples

The traceability clause should require lot numbering and batch records that let the buyer connect a specific drum to a specific COA and production batch. This supports the EU traceability obligations under Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 and the 로트 추적 체크리스트 that quality teams already run. The clause should also address retained samples: who keeps them, for how long, and how they are identified, so a later dispute can be tested against the same physical evidence.

6. Packaging, labeling, storage, and shelf life

PS is supplied in bulk packaging such as 25 kg net per drum, with a standard Nutranexa MOQ of 25 kg. The agreement should confirm the packing unit, inner packaging, labeling content, palletizing, and any destination-market label requirements. It should also confirm storage conditions and shelf life so that the buyer's warehouse team can check drums on receipt rather than discovering a mismatch after stock is placed.

7. Audit and inspection rights

The agreement should state whether the buyer may audit the supplier's site and records, how much notice is required, and which records are in scope. Audit rights do not replace the buyer's own verification activities, but they make it possible to check manufacturing, cleaning, and quality-control evidence when needed. The manufacturer audit guide 그리고 annual supplier review describe what a structured audit and scorecard should cover.

8. Claims, remedies, and evidence

Finally, the agreement should define how quality claims are raised, what evidence each side must provide (sample, COA, batch records, retain sample), and the time window for filing a claim. This is the clause that turns an out-of-specification result into an orderly process instead of an argument. Keep it simple: the claim process should mirror the deviation process so the quality team knows exactly what to collect.

If your team is drafting a PS quality agreement and needs the current specification, COA sample, and certificate files to attach as exhibits, request the current document set before finalizing the contract.

A Practical Checklist Before You Sign or Renew

Use this checklist for a first PS quality agreement or for the annual renewal review:

  1. Confirm the exact product code, source route, target assay, and manufacturing site are named in the scope.
  2. Attach the current controlled specification with test basis and method references.
  3. Define COA content and confirm whether the buyer may rely on the supplier COA for incoming release.
  4. Confirm the supplier's change-notification commitment and the approval process for material changes.
  5. Confirm deviation reporting and corrective-action documentation requirements.
  6. Confirm lot numbering, batch records, and retained-sample policy.
  7. Confirm packaging, labeling, storage, and shelf-life expectations, including the 25 kg drum standard where relevant.
  8. Confirm audit rights and the records that are in scope.
  9. Define the claim process, evidence requirements, and filing window.
  10. Set the review cadence so the agreement is re-checked at the annual supplier review or whenever the specification changes.

피해야 할 흔한 실수

  • Signing a quality agreement that references a specification version the buyer has never seen.
  • Assuming a COA alone replaces supplier qualification evidence under 21 CFR 111.75.
  • Accepting a "no change without notice" promise with no defined notice window or approval step.
  • Treating the agreement as a substitute for sample evaluation, third-party testing, and incoming inspection.
  • Leaving traceability and retain-sample clauses out because "we always get a COA."
  • Forgetting to update the agreement when the source route, packaging, or destination market changes.

자주 묻는 질문

What is a supplier quality agreement for bulk ingredients?

A supplier quality agreement is a written document between buyer and supplier that defines product identity, specifications, test methods, COA obligations, change notification, traceability, audit rights, and claim handling for an ingredient such as phosphatidylserine. It is not a purchase order and it is not a certificate; it is the quality interface that governs every order under the relationship.

Is a supplier quality agreement required by US or EU law?

No specific form is required. US dietary supplement CGMPs under 21 CFR Part 111 and the food preventive-controls supply-chain program under 21 CFR Part 117 Subpart G create supplier qualification and verification obligations, and EU food law creates traceability and HACCP-based procedure obligations under Regulations (EC) No 178/2002 and (EC) No 852/2004. A quality agreement is a practical way to document how those obligations are met.

Can US supplement manufacturers rely on a PS supplier COA?

Yes, under conditions. For components other than identity of dietary ingredients, 21 CFR 111.75 allows reliance on a supplier COA if the supplier is qualified by confirming the reliability of its COA results, the COA states methods, limits, and actual results, the qualification is documented, and the COA is periodically re-confirmed with quality control review.

What change-notification clauses do PS buyers need?

The clause should require advance notice of any change that could affect the ingredient, including source, process, equipment, specification, packaging, or site changes. It should define the notice window, the approval process, and the records to be provided, so a change cannot arrive silently inside a shipment.

How does a quality agreement connect to COA and specification review?

The agreement attaches or references the current controlled specification and defines what each batch COA must contain. That makes the COA review and specification and certificate review repeatable for every batch instead of depending on memory or informal email exchanges.

What evidence should a buyer collect when a PS batch is out of specification?

Collect the batch COA, the retained sample, the specification version in force at receipt, lot and shipment records, and the deviation report. The quality agreement should name exactly these items so the claim process starts with the right evidence.

결론

A supplier quality agreement does not replace COA review, certificate checks, audits, or testing. It makes those activities enforceable and repeatable by defining the specification, the COA, the change process, the traceability records, and the claim path in one controlled document. For PS buyers in the United States and the EU, the disciplined workflow is short: review the clauses against your own regulatory obligations, attach the current controlled specification and COA evidence, define change notification and deviation handling, and re-check the agreement at every annual review and specification revision.
