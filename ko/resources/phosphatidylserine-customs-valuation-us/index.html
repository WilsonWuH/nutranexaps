Phosphatidylserine Customs Valuation Review for US Importers

How US importers review phosphatidylserine customs valuation: transaction value, additions and exclusions, first-sale and related-party rules, duty math.

출판 August 12, 2026

PS 제품 보기규격서 요청

Every US entry for bulk 포스파티딜세린 (PS) rests on two separate customs decisions: tariff classification, which sets the duty rate, and customs value, which sets the base the rate is applied to. The direct answer for a US importer is to review the declared value as its own buying task: confirm that the transaction value is the price actually paid or payable for the PS when sold for exportation to the United States, add only the statutory amounts such as buyer-incurred packing costs and assists, keep international freight, insurance, and US duties out of the value when separately identified, verify whether a related-party or first-sale price needs extra support, and keep the invoice, packing list, payment records, and route-specific product file together, because CBP uses those records to appraise, classify, and assess duties. For PS under HTS 2923.20.20, the current general duty rate is 5 percent, so every dollar of declared value changes the duty paid. This article is for procurement, finance, logistics, and quality teams; it is not legal or customs advice, and the importer of record, customs broker, and current law control each entry.

Why Customs Valuation Is Its Own Buying Task

Valuation turns a commercial price into a legal duty base. It is easily confused with tasks beside it in the shipment calendar:

Import taskWhat it answersWhere it fits in the entry file
Tariff classificationWhich HTS code and duty rate applySets the rate; reviewed separately from value
Customs valuationWhat dollar base the rate applies toSets the duty base; the subject of this article
ISF filingAdvance cargo data before loadingFiled before arrival, uses booking data
FDA Prior NoticeFood-import notification for the shipmentFiled for FDA, not for value
FSVP evaluationImporter verification of the foreign supplierFood-safety duty, separate from value
Entry summaryThe release-and-duty filing itselfCBP Form 7501 reports classification and value together

그 HS code review explains why classification is route-specific. Valuation is just as fact-specific, but answers a different question: what number goes into the entry as the value of the goods. The practical trigger is the commercial invoice: the broker needs a defensible value that the customs broker handoff file supports. When the order moves through a trading company, distributor, or related supplier, the value question gets harder.

Transaction Value: The Baseline Method for PS Imports

What price actually paid or payable means

Under 19 U.S.C. § 1401a(b)(1), transaction value is the price actually paid or payable when sold for exportation to the United States, plus the statutory additions in the same section. 19 CFR § 152.103(a) adds that the price is considered without regard to how it was derived: it may include discounts, negotiations, formula pricing, or indirect payments.

Three practical points matter for a PS order:

  1. Use the price the US buyer pays, not the resale price. A later mark-up for US distribution is not part of the value.
  2. An indirect payment is still part of the price. If the buyer pays a supplier's debt, or a price reduction settles an earlier obligation, the total economic value can be the price actually paid or payable.

The five statutory additions

Transaction value is not simply the invoice line. Under 19 U.S.C. § 1401a(b)(1)(A)-(E), the price is increased by the following amounts, to the extent they are not already included and sufficient information exists:

AdditionWhat it covers for a PS buyer
Packing costsCosts incurred by the buyer for packing the imported merchandise
Selling commissionsCommissions incurred by the buyer in the sale
AssistsMaterials, components, tools, dies, molds, or engineering work supplied by the buyer for production
Royalty or license feesFees the buyer must pay as a condition of the sale for export to the US
Proceeds of resaleAny part of a later resale that accrues to the seller

What is excluded from transaction value

Under 19 U.S.C. § 1401a(b)(3) and (b)(4)(A), excluded from transaction value when identified separately are reasonable post-importation construction, erection, assembly, maintenance, or transportation costs and the customs duties and other federal taxes payable by reason of importation. International freight and insurance from the country of exportation to the place of importation are also excluded from the price. That is why the US value base differs from a delivered-cost estimate: if the seller quotes CIF or CIP, the invoice may include ocean freight and insurance, but those charges can be excluded from the customs value when identified separately. The marine cargo insurance review 그리고 Incoterms planning articles cover the commercial side; for value, keep the entry-value calculation separate from the landed-cost estimate.

Related-Party and First-Sale Questions PS Buyers Actually Face

Related-party purchases

When the buyer and seller are related, transaction value is still usable, but it must pass one of two tests under 19 U.S.C. § 1401a(b)(2)(B) 그리고 19 CFR § 152.103(l): the circumstances of sale show the relationship did not influence the price, or the value closely approximates test values such as the transaction value of identical or similar merchandise sold to unrelated US buyers, or a deductive or computed value for identical or similar merchandise. CBP's related-party informed compliance publication states the same rule. PS buyers most often meet this question through an affiliated distributor, a sister trading company, or a group supplier arrangement. The fix is to document why the price is right, with the purchase order, payment records, and, where relevant, a comparison to sales of identical or similar merchandise.

First-sale valuation in a trading-company chain

Many PS imports from China move in two sales: the manufacturer sells to an exporter or trading company, which sells to the US importer at a higher price. Under the first-sale doctrine, an importer may declare the value based on the first sale rather than the second-sale price, when the first sale is a bona fide arm's-length sale, the goods are clearly destined for the United States, and the price is settled in good faith. The standard is strict: in CBP ruling H347879, CBP said it is the importer's responsibility to show the first-sale price is acceptable under the standard in Nissho Iwai American Corp. v. United States, 982 F.2d 513 (Fed. Cir. 1992). A first-sale file needs the factory invoice or contract, the exporter's invoice, evidence the goods were sold for export to the US, and payment records for both levels.

SituationTypical treatment
Direct purchase from the manufacturer, unrelated partiesInvoice price plus statutory additions
Purchase through an unrelated trading company, direct evidence of both salesFirst-sale value may be supportable with a complete file
Purchase through a related exporter or distributorRelated-party tests apply
Consignment, sample, or no sale for exportTransaction value may not apply; sequential methods govern

The first-sale decision should be made with the broker before the entry; if the file is incomplete, the safer default is the price the US importer actually pays.

Building a PS Customs Valuation File

What the invoice must show

Under 19 U.S.C. § 1481, an invoice for merchandise imported into the US must set forth the port of entry; the parties and the time and place of sale; a detailed description including commercial name, grade or quality, and marks and numbers; quantities; the purchase price in the currency of purchase; the kind of currency; all charges itemized where known; and all rebates, drawbacks, and bounties allowed on exportation. For PS, that legal list maps directly to a buyer checklist:

Invoice fieldWhat a PS buyer should verify
Commercial descriptionMatches the route and the specification and COA wording
Grade and assayMatches the quoted product, e.g., a 20% or 50% PS grade, and the packing list
Marks and numbersMatch the drum labels, packing list, and bill of lading
Quantity and unitsDrum count and net weight per drum
Purchase price and currencyThe price actually paid or payable in the stated currency
ChargesFreight, insurance, packing, and commissions itemized or stated as included
Rebates and drawbacksItemized when allowed on exportation

The same discipline supports incoming inspection and warehouse release 그리고 로트 추적성.

Assists: the least-expected value line

An assist is anything supplied by the buyer, free of charge or at reduced cost, for use in the production or sale for export of the imported merchandise. Under 19 U.S.C. § 1401a(h)(1)(A), assists include materials, components, and parts incorporated in the merchandise; tools, dies, and molds used in production; merchandise consumed in production; and engineering, development, artwork, design work, and plans undertaken outside the United States. Under 19 CFR § 152.103(d)-(e), the assist value is added to the price and apportioned in a reasonable manner consistent with generally accepted accounting principles.

For a PS ingredient, assists are uncommon but possible, for example buyer-supplied tooling or engineering work abroad for a custom grade. Ask before entry whether the buyer supplied anything for production, and document the answer.

Duty Calculation and Additional Duties

The current HTS shows 2923.20.20 ("Other" phosphoaminolipids) with a general duty rate of 5 percent. The rate is only part of the check: importers must also verify whether the goods are subject to additional duties in Chapter 99, such as the Section 301 remedy on certain products of China. CBP's Section 301 FAQ explains that the additional duties apply to articles that are products of the People's Republic of China, that they are based on country of origin rather than country of export, and that exclusion processes are administered by USTR. Whether a specific PS product is covered is a classification-and-origin question the broker should confirm before filing; this article does not assert a Section 301 outcome for any product.

Recordkeeping and Penalties

Records must survive for five years

Under 19 CFR § 163.4, records required for CBP examination must be kept for 5 years from the date of entry, or 5 years from the date of the activity that required the record. For a PS import, that means the commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order, payment records, broker entry summary, bill of lading, and the product file supporting the description should be retained for the full period, not just until the lot is released.

What happens when the value is wrong

Under 19 U.S.C. § 1592, importation by means of material false statements, documents, or omissions is subject to civil penalties based on culpability. For fraud, the maximum penalty is the domestic value of the merchandise; for gross negligence, the lesser of the domestic value or four times the loss of revenue; for negligence, the lesser of the domestic value or two times the loss of revenue. Duties of which the US has been deprived must be restored regardless of penalty.

Common Mistakes in PS Valuation Review

  1. Treating the broker's draft value as final without checking the invoice and payment chain.
  2. Using the resale price, landed cost, or a CIF total as if it were the customs value.
  3. Copying a value from a previous shipment when the route, grade, or supplier changed.
  4. Assuming "trading company" means first-sale valuation is available.
  5. Overlooking buyer-supplied packing, tooling, or engineering that could be an assist.
  6. Mixing classification, ISF, Prior Notice, and FSVP files into one decision.
  7. Releasing the five-year record file too early because the lot was accepted.

결론

Customs valuation is a US buying task, not a broker detail. Confirm the transaction value, add only the statutory amounts, exclude identified international freight and insurance, and check the current HTS rate and any Chapter 99 additional duties before the entry summary is filed. Keep the invoice, packing list, payment records, and route-specific product file for five years, and treat related-party and first-sale questions as document exercises, not assumptions. A clean value file makes duty math predictable and audits less stressful.

자주 묻는 질문

What is the customs value of a phosphatidylserine shipment?

Transaction value: the price actually paid or payable for the PS when sold for exportation to the United States, plus statutory additions such as buyer-incurred packing costs and assists, excluding separately identified international freight, insurance, and US duties. The broker reports it on the entry summary.

Which costs must be added to the PS invoice price for US customs?

Packing costs incurred by the buyer, selling commissions, the value of assists, royalty or license fees required as a condition of sale, and proceeds of any resale accruing to the seller, to the extent not already included and supported by sufficient information.

Can a US importer use the first-sale price when buying PS through a trading company?

Yes, but only with a complete file. CBP requires the importer to show the first sale was a bona fide arm's-length sale, the goods were clearly destined for the United States, and the price was settled in good faith (Nissho Iwai; CBP ruling H347879).

Do related-party PS purchases need special valuation review?

Yes. Transaction value remains usable when the relationship did not influence the price or when the value closely approximates test values for identical or similar merchandise. Document the circumstances of sale and payment chain before entry.

What documents should the broker receive for PS valuation?

The commercial invoice meeting 19 U.S.C. 1481 requirements, packing list, purchase order, payment records, route-specific spec and COA, drum marks, and any related-party or first-sale support. Keep the set for five years under 19 CFR 163.4.

What are the risks of declaring the wrong PS customs value?

Material false statements or omissions can lead to civil penalties based on negligence, gross negligence, or fraud, plus restoration of lost duties. Prompt prior disclosure may reduce exposure.
