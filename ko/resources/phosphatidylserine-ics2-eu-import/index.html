Phosphatidylserine ICS2 and Entry Summary Declarations: EU Import Readiness for Bulk Buyers

Prepare phosphatidylserine ocean shipments for the EU Import Control System 2: who files the entry summary declaration, what data is needed, and when.

출판 August 11, 2026

PS 제품 보기규격서 요청

The direct answer for a European buyer of bulk 포스파티딜세린 is this: before a container is loaded for the EU, make sure a complete Entry Summary Declaration (ENS) has been lodged through the EU Import Control System 2 (ICS2), and give your forwarder or carrier the exact consignee, seller, goods description, goods code, weight, and value data before the shipping-instructions cut-off. EU customs authorities run safety and security risk analysis on every consignment based on that pre-arrival data; incomplete or inaccurate data can be rejected, can trigger a "do not load" instruction, or can delay release after the vessel arrives.

This guide explains who files the ENS on an ocean shipment, which data elements the importer must control, the time limits that drive the booking, and how to turn the process into a repeatable pre-booking checklist. The ENS is the safety-and-security layer of an EU import; it is separate from customs clearance, specification and COA 검토, and finished-product compliance. A filed ENS does not release goods for free circulation, and it does not prove that a batch meets your specification.

What ICS2 Is and What Changed for Sea Freight

A pre-arrival safety and security system, not a customs declaration

ICS2 is the European Commission's advance cargo information system. According to the Commission, all economic operators that bring goods to the EU or transit goods through the EU must declare safety and security data to ICS2 through the Entry Summary Declaration. Based on the ENS, all goods are subject to safety and security risk analysis and better targeted controls. The system is designed to identify high-risk consignments early and intervene at the most appropriate point in the supply chain while facilitating the legitimate flow of goods.

The legal anchor is the Union Customs Code. Article 127 of Regulation (EU) No 952/2013 requires that goods brought into the customs territory of the Union are covered by an entry summary declaration. Article 128 provides that customs carry out risk analysis, primarily for security and safety purposes, on the basis of that declaration, and Article 129 covers amendment and invalidation after lodgement. Detailed time limits are set out in Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/2446.

Buyers sometimes confuse the ENS with the import declaration used at clearance. The ENS is pre-arrival security-and-safety information filed before the goods are loaded or before arrival; the import declaration, handled through a customs agent or broker with your EORI-linked authorisation, comes later at the border. The ENS also has nothing to do with food-import controls or finished-product rules; it is one independent layer of the import file.

Release 3 brings maritime and land modes into scope

ICS2 has been rolled out in releases. Air, express, and postal consignments came first, and a later release extended the system to goods moved by maritime and inland waterway transport, road, and rail. For an ingredient importer in Europe, the practical consequence is that ocean shipments of phosphatidylserine from China have been inside the system since the maritime phase of the rollout, which ran through 2024 and 2025.

The Commission's ICS2 page also confirms a 2026 milestone: as of 1 June 2026, the temporary derogation related to the New Computerised Transit System (NCTS) Phase 6 ended, so all consignments entering the EU by any means of transport should have a valid ENS, filed directly in ICS2 or as part of a combined transit declaration through NCTS Phase 6 in opt-in countries. For a bulk PS shipment by sea, the ENS obligation is the normal operating condition of the booking, not an optional extra.

Who Files the ENS and What the Importer Must Supply

Carrier, house filer, or importer: agree the filing owner first

The Commission states that, depending on the contractual arrangements among supply chain actors and the availability of information, either single or multiple ENS filings may be used. In practice, that means the filing responsibility can sit in different places:

  • The sea carrier can file the ENS at the master bill-of-lading level for the whole vessel.
  • A freight forwarder or NVOCC acting as house-level filer can file at the house bill-of-lading level, which is how most consolidated or forwarded PS shipments are handled.
  • In some arrangements, the importer, a customs agent, or another economic operator files directly, using the Shared Trader Portal (STP) or its own system connected through the Shared Trader Interface (STI).

The European importer does not need to operate the filing software to be responsible for the data. The Commission notes that economic operators connecting to ICS2 need an EORI number from one of the EU Member States' customs authorities, and that filings can be submitted through the Shared Trader Portal. For most PS buyers, the practical owner of the filing is the carrier or forwarder, while the importer owns the quality of the underlying data. Your booking confirmation should state explicitly who files the ENS, at which level, and when shipping instructions must be submitted.

The data package the exporter and importer must align

The ENS data set covers the parties, the transport, and the goods: consignor, consignee, carrier, transport equipment and route, goods description, goods code, gross weight, and shipment references. For a phosphatidylserine shipment, align these elements before the cut-off:

  • Exact commercial product identity. Use the source-specific product description from the purchase order and specification, such as soy phosphatidylserine or sunflower phosphatidylserine, instead of a vague "PS powder" line. The description on the shipping instructions, commercial invoice, packing list, and 로트 추적성 records should agree.
  • Goods code. The ENS uses the harmonised-system classification, so the goods code should be confirmed with your customs specialist or broker rather than guessed. The HS-code review workflow for US and Canada and the EU customs-clearance checklist both explain why classification precision matters; the same discipline applies to the data you hand to the forwarder.
  • Weights. Provide net and gross weight per drum and per shipment. With a 25 kg net per drum baseline for PS, a 20-drum order is 500 kg net plus packaging; the forwarder needs the gross figure that will appear on the bill of lading and the packing list.
  • Parties. Confirm the consignee's legal name, address, and EORI where relevant, the consignor and seller details from the commercial invoice, and the notify party.
  • References. Give the purchase order, contract, and intended shipment dates so the forwarder can map the ENS to the correct booking.

One controlled source of truth prevents most ENS errors. Use the approved commercial file set as the master: specification and COA reference for product identity, commercial invoice and packing list for weights and parties, and booking confirmation for transport details. If the forwarder asks for data that does not match those files, stop and reconcile before the cut-off.

The Deadlines That Control the Booking

For sea transport, the lodgement deadlines come from the Union Customs Code's delegated rules, and they are not negotiable through the forwarder:

Mode or routeENS time limit (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/2446, Article 105)What the importer controls
Containerised cargo (typical PS shipment)At the latest 24 hours before the goods are loaded onto the vesselShipping-instruction data to the carrier or house filer before their internal cut-off, which is normally days before loading
Bulk or break-bulk cargoAt the latest 4 hours before the vessel arrives at the first EU port of entrySame data, but with less time between filing and arrival
Short-sea routes from Greenland, the Faeroe Islands, Iceland, Baltic, North Sea, Black Sea, Mediterranean ports, or Moroccan portsAt the latest 2 hours before arrivalConfirmation that the ENS was accepted before departure

For a European buyer, the deadline that controls your day-to-day work is the forwarder's shipping-instruction cut-off, not the 24-hour legal minimum. Most ocean bookings require complete instructions several days before the vessel's cut-off so the carrier and, where applicable, the house filer can file in time. Treat the legal limit as the backstop and the forwarder's cut-off as the working deadline.

What Happens When the Data Is Wrong or Late

The Commission is explicit about the consequences. EU customs authorities may reject incomplete ENS declarations or issue risk-mitigating referrals in the pre-loading or pre-arrival phase requesting the declarant to provide the necessary data. A referral can ask for additional information, for high-risk screening of the cargo, or in the most serious cases instruct the declarant not to load the cargo. The referral must be answered before the risk assessment can resume. The Commission also notes that incomplete data can create delays in processing the ENS and in the entry process after arrival, and that customs may impose administrative sanctions for non-compliance with data requirements.

For a PS shipment, a realistic escalation path looks like this:

  1. Before the vessel sails, ask the forwarder for the ENS status: lodged, accepted, or referred.
  2. If a referral is open, request the specific data point in writing and provide it immediately, together with the supporting file (commercial invoice line, packing-list weight, or goods description).
  3. If the response is "do not load", do not override the instruction. Hold the container and work through the forwarder and, if needed, the customs agent.
  4. After arrival, keep the ENS reference with the shipment file so the pre-shipment inspection 그리고 warehouse release teams can link the pre-arrival record to the actual goods.

An ENS issue is not a product-quality finding. It says nothing about the PS assay, contaminants, or specification compliance, so it should never be conflated with COA or third-party testing results. It is a data-quality problem in the import chain, and it is fixed with data.

A Practical Pre-Booking Checklist for PS Importers

Turn the workflow above into a checklist that any team member can run before every ocean booking:

  • Confirm in writing who files the ENS: carrier master-level, house filer, or importer-direct, and at which cut-off.
  • Validate the goods description against the approved product identity for the exact 두유 PS 또는 해바라기 PS route.
  • Confirm the goods code with the customs agent or broker and keep the classification note with the shipment file.
  • Reconcile net and gross weights across the commercial invoice, packing list, and booking confirmation, drum by drum.
  • Check consignee legal name, address, and EORI, plus seller and consignor details, against the contract and invoice.
  • Submit shipping instructions before the forwarder's cut-off and request written ENS acceptance.
  • Record the ENS reference or acceptance number with the 로트 추적성 and dispatch records.
  • Review the ENS status again before the vessel departs and resolve any referral before arrival.
  • Keep the pre-arrival record separate from customs clearance, and hand both to the customs-clearance file when the shipment arrives.

The same checklist works for first orders and repeat orders. The MOQ and packaging baseline of 25 kg per drum keeps the weight math simple, and the export documentation workflow already covers the commercial and packaging documents that feed the same data set.

자주 묻는 질문

What is ICS2 and does it apply to a phosphatidylserine shipment?

ICS2 is the EU's advance cargo information system. All economic operators bringing goods to the EU or transiting them through the EU must declare safety and security data through an Entry Summary Declaration. It applies to all modes, and for maritime transport it has been phased in since the 2024-2025 rollout, so an ocean shipment of bulk phosphatidylserine to Europe is inside the system.

Who files the entry summary declaration on an ocean PS shipment?

The filing responsibility depends on the contractual arrangements. The sea carrier may file at master bill-of-lading level, a forwarder or NVOCC may file as a house-level filer, or another economic operator may file directly through the Shared Trader Portal. The importer should confirm who files, at which level, and by which cut-off in writing at booking.

What data does the importer need to provide for an ENS?

The importer should provide the exact goods description, the confirmed goods code, net and gross weights, consignee details including EORI where relevant, seller and consignor details, and the shipment references. The data should match the commercial invoice, packing list, specification, and booking confirmation.

How many hours before loading must the ENS be lodged for a container?

For containerised cargo brought into the EU by sea, the entry summary declaration must be lodged at the latest 24 hours before the goods are loaded onto the vessel, unless a shorter short-sea time limit applies. The forwarder's shipping-instruction cut-off is usually earlier, so it should be confirmed in writing.

What happens if an ENS is rejected or incomplete?

EU customs authorities may reject incomplete declarations or issue risk-mitigating referrals that can request more data, screening, or a "do not load" instruction. The referral must be answered before the risk assessment can resume, and non-compliance can also lead to administrative sanctions and delays after arrival.

결론

ICS2 and the entry summary declaration have turned shipment data into a fixed part of exporting phosphatidylserine to Europe. The system is live for sea freight, the legal time limits are clear, and the cost of a rejected or referred ENS is measured in loading delays and arrival friction. The winning habit for an importer is simple: fix the filing owner at booking, prepare one reconciled data package from the commercial file set, submit before the forwarder's cut-off, and keep the ENS reference with the shipment file until the goods are released. That routine protects the first container and the hundredth, and it costs almost nothing compared with the delay it prevents.
