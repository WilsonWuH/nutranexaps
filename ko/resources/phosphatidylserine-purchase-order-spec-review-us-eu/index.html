Phosphatidylserine Purchase Order Specification Review: What US and EU Buyers Should Lock In

How to lock specifications, lot designation, packaging, documents, and inspection points into a phosphatidylserine purchase order.

출판 August 14, 2026

PS 제품 보기규격서 요청

The direct answer for a buyer is to treat the purchase order as the specification document for the transaction: name the exact product, source route, grade and assay, and specification version; fix quantity in 25 kg drum units; state packaging, Incoterms, dates, and lot designation rules; require a COA and packing documents per lot at shipment; reserve inspection and acceptance rights; and put change control in writing before the PO is issued. A PO that only states a price and a product name leaves the delivered lot open to interpretation. A PO that carries the agreed specification gives receiving, quality, and finance one document to check against.

This guide walks through the fields that belong on a bulk phosphatidylserine (PS) purchase order, the documents the PO should require, and the inspection and change-control points that protect a US or EU buyer from quotation to warehouse release. It is operational purchasing guidance, not legal advice.

Why the Purchase Order Is a Specification Document

Procurement teams often treat the PO as a back-office form, but for an ingredient like PS the PO is where the commercial and technical records meet. Before the PO, the specification lives in quotation documents, sample COAs, and email exchanges. After the PO, it becomes the reference point for production, dispatch, receiving, payment, and any dispute.

There are three practical reasons to treat the PO as a specification document:

  • The PO fixes what is being bought. Under the US Uniform Commercial Code, Article 2 governs transactions in goods; a PO that names product, quantity, price, and delivery creates the enforceable baseline quality and finance use later.
  • The spec is what the COA is measured against. A certificate of analysis reports results against a specification and a test basis. Without the spec version on the order, a supplier and buyer can disagree about whether a result is a pass.
  • The PO is the audit trail. Regulators, customers, and internal quality systems ask where requirements came from. The PO is the document that shows the buyer stated the requirement in writing before the goods were made.

Locking the Specification Version

The most common PO failure is not a missing specification, but an unversioned one. A spec sheet is a controlled document: it has a number, a revision, an issue date, and a set of parameters with typical or guaranteed values. When the PO says "per attached spec PS-S-2026-08 Rev B," both sides know exactly which document governs. When it says only "food grade," the COA comparison is ambiguous.

For US buyers, this mirrors the dietary supplement CGMP rule in 21 CFR Part 111: 21 CFR 111.70 requires established component specifications, 111.75 requires identity verification, and the PO is the buyer-side mirror that states the requirement in writing.

For PS specifically, verify three things on the specification before it goes on the PO:

  • Assay and grade. Nutranexa's PS range includes 20%, 50%, and 70% grades, so the PO must name the exact grade. A 50% grade COA compared against a 20% spec is meaningless.
  • Test basis. The analytical method or standard should be named. Public sample COAs for Nutranexa PS are issued on the QB/T 5821-2023 test basis, giving receiving a reference method to check.
  • Typical versus guaranteed values. Specifications distinguish typical results from limits that must be met. The PO should reference the limits, not the typical values, for acceptance decisions.

Keep the current version in the documents for PS ingredients file, and treat any revision after the PO as a change requiring written confirmation, not a silent update.

Quantity, Packaging, and Lot Designation

Quantity in drum units

Bulk PS is sold and shipped in drums, so the PO should state both total weight and drum count. Nutranexa's baseline for PS is a 25 kg minimum order quantity with 25 kg net per drum. A PO that says "500 kg" without drum units creates a counting problem at loading and receiving; a PO that says "20 drums of 25 kg net" is verifiable against the packing list and the truck or container tally.

Lot designation

A lot designation is the batch identifier that ties an order to a specific production run. It belongs on the PO as a requirement, even when the number is assigned later: state that each drum carries a lot number, that the COA reports that exact lot, and that the buyer will be notified before any substitution. This is what makes 로트 추적성 work after arrival.

For EU buyers, traceability has a legal anchor. Regulation (EC) No 178/2002, Article 18 requires food business operators to be able to identify any person from whom food was supplied and any business to which it was supplied, the one-step-back, one-step-forward rule. A PO that requires lot numbers on drums and documents gives the importer the records needed to satisfy that requirement. In practice, GS1 application identifiers encode the lot number with the product identifier on labels and barcodes, which is the common way trading partners exchange batch information electronically.

One drum, one lot, one label

The cleanest control is one drum, one lot, one label: mixed-lot drums break the COA-to-goods linkage and complicate sampling, reserve samples, and complaints. Confirm the drum label format before dispatch so it matches the lot rules the PO established.

Inspection and Acceptance Points

Before dispatch

그 pre-shipment inspection checklist covers the factory-side check: drums sealed, labels correct, lot numbers matching the packing list, and dispatch records complete. The PO should reserve the buyer's right to schedule or witness this check; fixing a problem at the factory costs far less than after the goods cross the border.

At receiving

그 incoming inspection and warehouse release procedure is where the PO becomes operational. The receiving team verifies drum count, lot numbers, labels, seals, and the COA against the PO. Under US law, the UCC gives the buyer a right to inspect goods before payment or acceptance; the PO should state that right explicitly, allowing the buyer to hold goods in quarantine, draw samples, and verify identity under 21 CFR 111.75 before release to inventory.

The three-way match

Finance closes the loop with a three-way match: the PO, the goods receipt, and the commercial invoice must agree on product, quantity, price, and terms. A drum count that matches the packing list but not the PO, or an invoice naming a different Incoterms rule, is a control failure even when the powder is fine. The supplier quality agreement sets the ongoing rules; the three-way match verifies they were followed.

Change Control After the PO Is Placed

Orders change: quantities move, dates slip, sources swap, spec sheets revise. The PO should state that any change requires a written request, a revised PO or addendum, and supplier confirmation before production. Two changes deserve special attention:

  • Source-route changes. A switch from soy PS to sunflower PS, or a change of manufacturing site, changes the raw-material chain and the documents that go with it. The buyer should treat this as a new qualification question, not a clerical edit.
  • Specification revisions. If the supplier issues a revised spec after the PO, the buyer should confirm whether the delivered lot will be produced to the old or new version, and what that means for the agreed price and acceptance limits.

그 repeat-order document review explains how to keep recurring orders controlled, and the annual supplier review is where order-level lessons should feed back into supplier scoring. The PO change-control clause is what makes those reviews actionable.

US and EU Order-Placement Checklist

Before issuing a PS purchase order:

  1. Confirm the quotation matches the PO: product, grade, source route, price, Incoterms, and lead time.
  2. Attach or reference the current specification version and its issue date.
  3. State total quantity, drum count, and the 25 kg net-per-drum basis.
  4. Require lot numbers on every drum and a COA reporting the exact lot.
  5. Specify packaging, sealing, and labeling expectations.
  6. List the documents that must accompany the shipment: COA, packing list, commercial invoice, and any origin or certificate files.
  7. Reserve inspection rights before dispatch and at receiving, with a hold-and-release procedure.
  8. State the change-control rule: no change without written request and confirmation.
  9. Reference the quality agreement or confirm one is in place.
  10. Keep the PO, spec, and quotation in one order file for the three-way match and future audits.

자주 묻는 질문

What should a purchase order for phosphatidylserine include?

A PS purchase order should include product identity, source route, grade and assay, the specification version and issue date, quantity and drum count, packaging and sealing, Incoterms and destination, dates, required documents such as the COA and packing list, inspection rights, and a change-control rule.

Do supplement ingredient purchase orders need the specification attached?

Yes, as a matter of control. The specification version defines what the COA is measured against and what receiving can accept. Attach the current spec sheet or reference its number and issue date, and treat any later revision as a change requiring written confirmation.

Should a PS purchase order require a COA for every lot?

Yes. Each lot should arrive with a COA reporting the assay, specification version, test basis, and the exact lot number on the drums. Without it, receiving cannot verify the shipment against the PO.

What is a lot designation on a bulk ingredient order?

A lot designation is the batch identifier assigned to a production run. On a PO it is a requirement: each drum carries a lot number, the COA reports that lot, and the supplier must notify the buyer before any substitution. This supports one-step-back, one-step-forward traceability under EU food law.

What is the difference between a purchase order and a quality agreement?

A purchase order is the commercial transaction document for one order: product, quantity, price, terms, and documents. A quality agreement sets the ongoing relationship rules: specifications, changes, deviations, document requirements, and dispute handling. They should reference each other, with the PO carrying the transaction-specific fields.

What happens if the delivered PS does not match the PO specification?

The buyer should hold the lot in quarantine, verify the COA and drum labels against the PO, draw retain samples, and notify the supplier in writing, following the inspection and change-control procedure in the PO and quality agreement. The UCC inspection right supports verifying goods before acceptance.

결론

The purchase order is where a PS quotation becomes a controlled transaction. Locking product identity, source route, grade and assay, specification version, quantity in drum units, lot designation, packaging, Incoterms, dates, documents, and inspection rights into the PO gives every downstream team a single reference: receiving checks the packing list, quality checks the COA against the spec, and finance matches the invoice. Change control keeps the order stable, and traceability records keep it auditable in both markets. Buyers who write the PO like a specification document avoid the most common failure mode of ingredient purchasing: not a bad lot, but an unclear baseline.
