## Introduction

Turkey rarely appears first in a phosphatidylserine market-entry spreadsheet, yet the commercial case is harder to ignore than most buyers expect: a population approaching ninety million with a young median age alongside a fast-growing over-50 cohort, a supplement category expanding from sports nutrition into cognitive health and healthy aging, and a manufacturing base that assembles finished products for both domestic shelves and regional export. Turkey also sits in a unique regulatory position — a customs union with the EU for industrial goods, a food framework harmonized to a significant degree with European rules, but its own authority, the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry, running the border controls that every food import must pass. A PS grade that clears EU review can still stall at a Turkish border inspection post if the importer's registration, the labeling file, and the ingredient documents do not line up.

This guide is written for sourcing managers and brand owners evaluating Turkey for the first time. It walks through the Turkish Food Codex framework and the Ministry's role, the registration and customs-clearance steps your Turkish counterpart must complete, and the documentation to demand from your ingredient supplier before the first shipment. The recurring theme will be familiar: verify current requirements in official Turkish sources rather than assuming that European or regional clearances transfer automatically, because the details matter at the border. ## Main Content

### Why Turkey is a strategic PS market at the crossroads of Europe and Asia

Turkey's consumer health market rests on scale and geography: a large domestic consumer base, established logistics corridors into Central Asia, the Middle East, and the EU, and a domestic supplement manufacturing sector that imports raw materials and sells finished products across the region. The cognitive-health segment is earlier in its maturity curve than Japan or Western Europe, which cuts both ways — category education is still being built, but shelf competition for brain-health claims is thinner and retail partners are actively sourcing differentiated ingredients. Domestic production of specialty phospholipids is effectively limited; PS grades are import-dependent, typically arriving as powders or granules from Asian, European, or US suppliers through Turkish importers and contract manufacturers who carry the import paperwork. Landed-cost modeling should account for duties and clearance timelines realistically, because first-time shipments through border inspection posts often move slower than repeat shipments with an established compliance history. Buyers who treat Turkey as an extension of their EU plan usually discover the differences at the registration and labeling stage.

### The Turkish regulatory framework: Turkish Food Codex and Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry

Turkey regulates foods and food supplements primarily through the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry, with rules consolidated in the Turkish Food Codex (Türk Gıda Kodeksi), the harmonized body of food legislation published through the Official Gazette. Much of the Codex has been aligned with EU food law over the past two decades, which is good news for buyers with EU files: specification formats, allergen logic, and labeling structure will feel familiar. Food supplements are regulated as a distinct category under Codex rules modeled on the EU directive framework, with their own composition, labeling, and notification expectations rather than a pharmaceutical pathway — provided the product's presentation stays within food claims and does not drift toward medicinal positioning.

For an ingredient buyer, the strategic question is PS's status at the intended dose and claim set. Turkey's novel-ingredient treatment broadly tracks EU thinking, and phosphatidylserine's positioning in Europe has its own history; because Turkish implementation details and dose thresholds are not always published with the granularity of the EU register, verify the current treatment with the Ministry's published rules or a Turkish regulatory consultant before committing to artwork.

### Import requirements, business registration and customs clearance in Turkey

The practical gatekeeper in Turkey is the importer of record. Food import activities can only be conducted by a Turkish-registered food business whose scope covers importing: the counterpart company registers its food business activity with the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry through the provincial directorates, maintains the premises and traceability systems the food law requires, and operates through the customs system as a registered importer. Foreign suppliers do not register themselves — choosing a Turkish importer or distributor that handles food-category imports routinely is the single highest-leverage decision in the market entry plan, and who bears import risk should be written into the supply agreement.

Every PS shipment clears through a border inspection post where the Ministry's controls apply alongside standard customs procedures. In practice this means risk-based documentary checks — commercial invoice, bill of lading, certificate of origin, and the food safety documents accompanying the goods — with physical examination or laboratory sampling where the risk profile calls for it. Certain product categories and HS headings also sit under advance control-certificate or conformity schemes administered with Turkish authorities, including the Turkish Standards Institution (TSE) framework where standards apply, so confirm early whether your HS heading carries such a requirement; the checking discipline that keeps multi-market customs entries moving is described in our [EU customs clearance checklist for PS ingredients](/resources/phosphatidylserine-eu-customs-clearance-checklist/). On the quality side, expect Turkish counterparties — like EU buyers — to read per-batch COAs closely, and oxidative parameters in particular, a practice covered in our [PS oxidative stability testing guide](/resources/phosphatidylserine-oxidative-stability-testing/).

### Turkish-language labeling and specification expectations

Labeling is where Turkey diverges most visibly from a copy-paste EU plan: the Turkish Food Codex labeling rules require Turkish-language mandatory information on retail packs, following the format conventions published through the Official Gazette. The mandatory set will be familiar to EU-experienced buyers — product name, full ingredient list with allergen emphasis, nutrition declaration, net quantity, best-before date, storage conditions, and the Turkish importer's name and address, with origin indications where the rules require them. Soy-derived PS puts soy on the allergen line, exactly as in Europe, and sunflower-derived grades remove that declaration. Functional claims must stay inside what the food supplement framework permits; imported artwork that carries EU English-language claims verbatim will need Turkish adaptation and a compliance review, not just translation. The ingredient document pack behind the label is the same backbone used for US and EU shipments — a current specification stating identity and assay, per-batch COAs, allergen and GMO declarations, and manufacturing site certification — and consistency across those documents is what reviewers and border officials cross-check. Build the Turkish label file as a dedicated workstream with its own timeline rather than as a translation afterthought.

## FAQ

### Q: Does phosphatidylserine require pre-market registration in Turkey?

**A:** The ingredient itself is not registered separately by the supplier, but the finished food supplement must comply with the Turkish Food Codex rules for its category, and PS's status at the intended dose and claim set should be verified against current Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry guidance before launch. Because Turkish novel-ingredient treatment broadly follows EU thinking but is not always published at the same granularity, confirm the positioning with a Turkish regulatory consultant where the claim set is aggressive.

### Q: Can a foreign company import PS into Turkey directly?

**A:** No. Food import activities must be carried out by a Turkish-registered food business whose registered scope covers importing, operating through the customs system as the importer of record. Foreign suppliers work with Turkish importers, distributors, or trading partners, and should contractually clarify who registers the activity, files customs paperwork, and bears import risk.

### Q: Which supplier documents does a Turkish importer need for PS?

**A:** The core pack is the specification, per-batch COA, SDS, allergen and GMO declarations, manufacturing site quality certification, and any health or origin certificates the border inspection post requests for the HS heading. Consistency across all documents — names, grades, sites, and quantities — matters as much as completeness, because mismatches are what trigger physical inspection and sampling.

### Q: How long does Turkey market entry take for a PS supplement?

**A:** Timelines vary with partner readiness and claim scope: registering the importer's food business activity where not already in place, adapting artwork to Turkish-language labeling rules, and clearing the first shipment through border controls each add weeks. Planning the ingredient document pack in parallel with importer selection — rather than after it — is the most reliable way to compress the calendar.
