## Introduction

Payment terms decide when your money leaves your account, which documents move with it, and what you can do if something goes wrong between contract and delivery. For a European buyer of bulk [phosphatidylserine](/products/phosphatidylserine/) from China, the direct answer is this: for a first bulk order, use the simplest structure that still protects both sides, usually a modest advance paid only after written order confirmation, with the balance released against shipping documents or after the agreed inspection gate. If order value, payment risk, or supplier familiarity justifies bank costs, a documentary letter of credit under the ICC UCP 600 rules is the standard alternative. Open account and full advance payment are possible, but both shift most of the risk to one side, so they should be chosen deliberately rather than accepted by default.

This guide covers the payment methods used in international ingredient trade, how to link a payment schedule to the document and inspection gates you already review, and which checks to complete before sending money. It also explains where the EU Late Payment Directive fits and what verified Nutranexa facts mean for your payment conversation. This is operational guidance, not legal or financial advice.

## Payment Terms vs. Incoterms: Two Different Decisions

Payment terms and delivery terms answer different questions, even though both appear on the same quotation. Incoterms, such as FCA, CIP, DAP, and DDP covered in our [phosphatidylserine Incoterms guide](/resources/phosphatidylserine-incoterms-europe/), define where goods are handed over, who arranges and pays for transport and insurance, and where risk transfers. Payment terms define how and when the buyer pays the seller, and which documents release each payment.

The two decisions interact. If you buy DAP and the seller controls the shipment until your named destination, the seller carries more performance risk and may reasonably ask for a payment structure that balances that exposure. If you buy FCA and take control at origin, you arrange the ocean leg and may want payment linked to the transport documents you hold. Record both sets of terms on the same order confirmation, with the Incoterms rule and named place stated separately from the payment method and milestones.

## The Payment Methods European Buyers Can Use

International trade guidance groups the common methods of payment into a small set: bank transfers such as T/T (telegraphic transfer), documentary letters of credit, documentary collections, open account, and bank guarantees. The U.S. International Trade Administration's country guides describe these options and their typical uses. Each method changes the risk balance between buyer and seller.

### T/T bank transfer

A T/T is a cross-border bank transfer, usually sent through the SWIFT network. It is fast and inexpensive relative to documentary methods, which is why it is common for sample and repeat orders. The buyer's protection comes from the schedule structure, not the mechanism: once a transfer is sent, recovering it depends on the seller's cooperation or contract enforcement. European buyers therefore combine T/T with milestones, such as a modest advance and the balance against shipping documents.

### Documentary letter of credit (UCP 600)

A documentary letter of credit is a bank arrangement under which the issuing bank gives a definite undertaking to pay the seller against a complying presentation of documents. The ICC's Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600) has governed letter-of-credit transactions worldwide for decades and includes the eUCP supplement for electronic presentation. Under UCP 600, a credit is irrevocable and banks examine documents, not the goods themselves.

Two points matter for ingredient buyers. First, an unconfirmed credit protects you mainly against the seller not performing: the seller is paid only when the documents match the credit exactly, and that does not confirm that the goods inside the container match the certificate of analysis. Second, a confirmed credit adds a second bank's undertaking to pay, relevant when the issuing bank's country or creditworthiness is a concern. Buyers pay for this in fees and document strictness, so the credit must be drafted around the exact documents you want to control, including your normal [COA and specification review](/resources/phosphatidylserine-coa-specification-certificate-review/) files.

### Documentary collection (URC 522)

Documentary collection uses banks as intermediaries without a bank payment undertaking. The seller ships the goods, then presents documents to its bank, which forwards them to the buyer's bank with a collection instruction. Under the ICC Uniform Rules for Collections (URC 522), every collection needs a collection instruction, and banks are not obliged to store or insure the goods. In documents-against-payment (D/P), the buyer pays to receive the documents; in documents-against-acceptance (D/A), the buyer accepts a draft, receives the documents, and pays later.

Collection sits between T/T and L/C in cost and protection, and can be a reasonable middle option with a known supplier, but banks do not guarantee payment or inspect the goods.

### Open account

Open account means the seller ships the goods and invoices the buyer, trusting the buyer to pay on the agreed date. U.S. trade guidance describes it as the most common method in mature, low-risk markets such as the Netherlands, but for a first import order it concentrates risk with the seller, which is why most suppliers will not offer it until a relationship is established. It becomes attractive after several clean repeat orders, once the supplier is approved in your [supplier onboarding](/resources/phosphatidylserine-supplier-onboarding/) file.

### Bank guarantees (URDG 758)

A bank guarantee is a separate instrument in which a bank undertakes to pay if the applicant fails to fulfil an obligation. Common types include advance payment guarantees, performance bonds, and maintenance bonds. For ingredient imports, an advance payment guarantee is the most relevant: if you pay a deposit, it can give you a mechanism to claim the deposit back if the supplier fails to deliver. The ICC Uniform Rules for Demand Guarantees (URDG 758) are the standard rule set; under these rules, demand guarantees are irrevocable by nature and independent of the underlying contract, so they are called only against the terms of the guarantee itself.

| Payment method | How it works | Main buyer risk | Main supplier risk | Best fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **T/T with milestones** | Bank transfer split into agreed stages | Advance lost if seller fails | Balance unpaid after shipment | Sample and first bulk orders |
| **Letter of credit** | Bank pays against complying documents | Fees and document strictness | Documents must match exactly | Larger orders, new relationships |
| **Documentary collection** | Banks route documents; payment on D/P or D/A | No bank guarantee of payment | Buyer may not pay/accept | Established supplier, mid value |
| **Open account** | Ship now, invoice, pay later | Low for buyer | Non-payment risk | Repeat orders, low risk |
| **Advance payment guarantee** | Bank backs the deposit | Guarantee terms and bank risk | Guarantee cost | Large deposits, first orders |

## Choosing Payment Terms by Order Stage

The right terms depend on where you are in the relationship, not only on the order value:

1. **Sample order.** Pay by T/T. Confirm the sample specification, packaging, and documents that will accompany it, so the first shipment is also a document test.
2. **First bulk order.** Start with a modest advance, then release the balance against shipping documents or after the agreed inspection gate. If the advance is material, evaluate a letter of credit or an advance payment guarantee.
3. **Repeat orders.** Once several lots are approved, negotiate terms that fit your cash flow, such as a lower advance, documentary collection, or later balance dates, while keeping the document triggers you already use.

Whatever the stage, write the terms down. A verbal "30 percent now, balance after shipment" becomes a dispute when the shipment is late. Capture payment expectations early in your [RFQ checklist for importers](/resources/phosphatidylserine-rfq-checklist-importers/), because the supplier's answer is part of its commercial profile alongside [MOQ, packaging, and lead time](/resources/phosphatidylserine-moq-packaging-lead-time/).

## Building a Payment Schedule Around the Quality Gate

The most practical way to reduce payment risk is to attach each milestone to a document or event you already verify:

| Milestone | Trigger | Typical documents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Advance** | Written order confirmation and proforma invoice accepted | Proforma invoice, order confirmation, payment terms |
| **Production complete** | Lot produced and batch-specific COA issued | Current COA, specification sheet, production confirmation |
| **Shipment ready** | Goods packed and inspected at origin | Packing list, commercial invoice, [pre-shipment inspection](/resources/phosphatidylserine-pre-shipment-inspection-checklist/) report |
| **Balance** | Shipping documents presented after loading | Bill of lading, insurance certificate if agreed, final invoice |
| **Post-delivery (optional)** | Goods received and checked at your warehouse | Receiving report, [incoming inspection](/resources/phosphatidylserine-incoming-inspection-warehouse-release/) records |

The exact split is a commercial negotiation, but the logic is not: each payment should buy a verifiable step, and each document should carry the same lot identity you use for [lot traceability](/resources/phosphatidylserine-lot-traceability-checklist/). If a supplier asks for a high advance before production starts, ask which documents prove production began and whether an advance payment guarantee is available. Keep the schedule consistent with your delivery terms so the named place and payment milestones do not contradict each other.

## Currency, Bank Fees, and EU Cash-Flow Rules

Currency choice is part of payment terms. Chinese suppliers commonly quote in U.S. dollars or euros, and the invoice currency decides who carries exchange-rate risk between quotation and payment. U.S. trade guidance explains that cash-in-advance in the agreed currency removes most currency exposure for the seller, while forward contracts and currency options are the standard tools when payment is deferred in a foreign currency. State the invoice currency in the quotation, check transfer and correspondent fees, and decide whether the price is fixed before payment.

Within the European Union, Directive 2011/7/EU on combating late payment sets the baseline for business-to-business payment periods: as a general rule, contractual payment periods are limited to 60 calendar days, with longer periods possible only if not grossly unfair to the creditor. Public authorities face a stricter norm: normally 30 days, at most 60. The directive also explicitly allows instalment payments. Two consequences matter for an importer: it governs payments inside the EU, not your contract with a supplier in China, so it does not cap the advance a Chinese supplier requests; and it shapes the terms your own EU customers expect, so your import schedule should be planned against your resale terms rather than funding a long customer window with a full advance.

## Verification Steps Before You Send Money

Before the first transfer, complete a short verification routine. These checks are cheap compared with a lost advance.

- Confirm the payee name on the bank details matches the legal entity on the business documents, the invoice, and the [production licence review](/resources/phosphatidylserine-food-production-license-review-us-eu/) file. A personal or third-party account is a serious warning sign.
- Ask for bank details on official stationery or in the order confirmation, and confirm the SWIFT/BIC and account fields with your bank before transferring.
- Never accept a last-minute change of bank account by email. If the account changes, require the change to come through a verified channel and be reflected in a revised order confirmation.
- Review the invoice against the quotation: unit price, quantity, source, grade, packaging, Incoterms rule, and named place.
- Confirm in writing which documents trigger each milestone.
- For a large advance, evaluate an advance payment guarantee issued under URDG 758 and confirm the issuing bank's standing with your own bank.
- Keep records of every payment instruction and confirmation, because they become part of your [repeat-order document review](/resources/phosphatidylserine-repeat-order-document-review/) and audit trail.

Payment structures cannot replace technical qualification. A supplier can be paid on perfect commercial documents while the goods still need a [COA review](/resources/phosphatidylserine-coa-specification-certificate-review/), [third-party testing](/resources/phosphatidylserine-third-party-testing-us-eu/), or [incoming inspection](/resources/phosphatidylserine-incoming-inspection-warehouse-release/) before release. Use payment terms to control cash flow and quality gates to control product risk; the two systems reinforce each other when they reference the same lot numbers.

## FAQ

### What payment terms do Chinese phosphatidylserine suppliers usually accept?

Common options are T/T with a structured schedule, documentary letters of credit, and documentary collection, depending on the supplier, order size, and relationship. Trade guidance describes letters of credit as a secure method frequently used when parties are less established, while T/T milestones suit sample and first orders. There is no universal split, so treat any quoted advance as a commercial condition and confirm it with the trigger documents in writing.

### Is a letter of credit worth the cost for a 25 kg drum order?

Often not. A single 25 kg drum is a small transaction, and letter-of-credit fees, amendment charges, and document strictness can absorb a meaningful share of the order value. For a first single-drum order, a T/T structure with a modest advance and the balance against shipping documents is usually more practical. As order value and payment exposure grow, a letter of credit becomes easier to justify.

### What documents should trigger each payment milestone?

The standard set is the commercial and proforma invoice for the advance, the batch-specific COA and specification sheet for production completion, the packing list and inspection report for shipment readiness, and the bill of lading with the final invoice for the balance. Keep the lot number identical across the COA, packing list, and transport documents so each milestone supports the same [traceability](/resources/phosphatidylserine-lot-traceability-checklist/) story.

### Should I pay the full amount before shipment?

Full advance payment is the highest-risk option for a buyer and should be avoided unless the amount is small, the supplier is already trusted, or the advance is backed by a bank guarantee. If a supplier requests a large advance, ask which production or document evidence will be provided before the balance, and consider an advance payment guarantee under URDG 758.

### Does the EU Late Payment Directive apply to my import contract with a Chinese supplier?

No. Directive 2011/7/EU applies to commercial transactions within the European Union, so it does not cap the payment period or advance requested by a supplier outside the EU. It still matters because it limits EU business-to-business payment periods to 60 calendar days as a general rule, with 30 days for public authorities, and it explicitly allows instalment payments. Your import schedule should stay compatible with the terms your EU customers expect.

## Conclusion

Payment terms are a procurement decision, not a formality. For European buyers of bulk phosphatidylserine, the practical pattern is to start with a modest, document-linked T/T structure for first orders, introduce documentary instruments as value and risk grow, and never let a payment milestone run ahead of the evidence you can verify. Written terms, verified bank details, and a schedule tied to the COA, inspection, and shipping documents turn payment into a controlled gate. Ask for payment terms in the same written package as the specification, COA, and delivery terms, and review them with the same discipline you apply to price and quality.
