## Introduction

Comparing phosphatidylserine quotations looks simple until three suppliers return prices per kilogram that differ by thirty percent for what appears to be the same powder. In practice, most of that gap comes from what each quote does and does not include: the assay basis behind the unit price, testing and documentation inclusions, packaging format, minimum order quantity, payment terms, and the Incoterm the price is built on. This guide walks US and EU procurement teams through a line-by-line method for dismantling a PS quote, normalizing every offer to a single comparison basis, and building a side-by-side matrix that reflects true landed cost rather than the headline number.

## What a Complete PS Quotation Should Contain

A quotation is only comparable if it is complete; a missing field is not a saving but a cost that surfaces later as a change order, a delay, or a compliance gap. A workable PS quotation should state, in writing:

- Product identity: botanical source, powder or liquid form, and declared PS assay with the referenced spec sheet revision.
- Price structure: unit price, currency, volume tiers, and the price validity window.
- Commercial terms: MOQ, lead time, Incoterm and named place, and payment terms.
- Inclusions: what testing, documents, and packaging are bundled into the price - and what is charged separately.
- Conditions: storage temperature, shelf life from date of manufacture, and surcharge triggers such as split deliveries or custom packaging.

If one supplier quotes FOB an Asian port and another DAP your European warehouse, the numbers answer different questions. Decide first which Incoterm the comparison runs at, and ask every supplier to restate their price on that basis.

## Normalize the Basis: Grade, Spec, and Assay

The most common comparison error is averaging prices across products that are not the same product. PS is sold in variations that change both cost and formulation performance:

- Source: soy- and sunflower-derived PS differ in raw material cost, allergen implications, and EU label positioning.
- Assay: a 50% and a 70% powder deliver different kilograms of PS per drum; the cheaper 50% material is often costlier per kilogram of active PS.
- Form: liquid concentrates and spray-dried powders differ in price, handling, and dosing needs.

Normalize every quote to price per kilogram of declared PS content, confirm whether the assay is as-is or moisture-adjusted, and check that each spec sheet reports the same parameters - assay method, peroxide value, heavy metals, microbiology, residual solvents - so quality claims compare like-for-like.

| Quote line | Why it matters | What to normalize |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source and grade | Drives allergen, label, and cost structure | Fix one source, or compare sources as separate scenarios |
| Declared assay | Determines cost per kg of active PS | Convert to price per kg of PS content |
| Moisture basis | Changes delivered yield | Confirm as-is vs dry-basis reporting |
| Specification revision | Defines the quality you are buying | Attach the exact spec version to the quote |

## Testing and Documentation Costs Behind the Price

Two quotes with identical unit prices can differ by thousands once testing and documentation are priced in. Suppliers bundle these differently, so ask three questions per offer:

- What is included per lot? A COA covering identity, assay, and core contaminants is usually included, but parameter lists vary - compare the COA template, not the promise of one.
- What is charged extra? Third-party testing, customer-specified methods, country-specific document packs, and statements such as allergen, GMO, irradiation, or TSE/BSE may be free or billed per document or shipment.
- Who pays for re-testing? If a quality agreement or EU official control requires confirmatory border testing, clarify whether the supplier contributes when their COA is disputed.

In the US these records feed your supplier-qualification file under dietary supplement GMP; EU importers need them for their own food-safety obligations. A quote that looks cheaper by excluding documents you will inevitably need has moved the cost to a later line item.

## Packaging, MOQ, and Payment Terms

Packaging, MOQ, and payment terms look commercial rather than technical, yet all three change the effective cost per kilogram.

Packaging: bulk PS powder normally ships as a sealed foil or multi-layer liner inside a fiber drum, often with nitrogen flush to protect an oxidation-sensitive phospholipid. Nitrogen flush versus a basic liner is a real difference: it affects shelf life and oxidation-claim risk.

MOQ: a low headline price on a large MOQ can be worse than a higher price on a quantity you can move. Model the comparison at the quantity you will genuinely order over twelve months, and price the cash and warehouse burden of the surplus. If you need split deliveries, ask whether the MOQ applies per shipment or per contract - surcharges can erase a price advantage.

Payment terms: advance transfer, partial advance against documents, and letters of credit carry different costs and risk. A supplier demanding a large advance is asking you to finance their working capital - price that in, or treat balanced terms as a negotiating concession.

## Logistics Terms and Hidden Costs

With quotes restated at the same Incoterm, the remaining differences sit between the supplier's gate and your warehouse. Build a landed-cost stack for every offer:

| Cost layer | Typical example | Notes for comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ocean or air freight | 20-ft container, EU or US port | Get quotes valid for the same shipping window |
| Insurance | Marine cargo cover | Often excluded from CIF quotes |
| Duty and taxes | HS-classified import duty, VAT | Confirm classification per lane, not per supplier |
| Customs brokerage and clearance | Broker fee, ISF or entry filing | Fixed cost, hits small orders hardest |
| Port and inland charges | Terminal handling, demurrage risk, inland trucking | Volatile; update quarterly |
| Receiving costs | Inbound testing, warehouse handling, sampling | Your internal standard cost |

Two hidden costs deserve attention. Time: a longer lead time forces safety stock, which carries cost. Failure risk: a supplier whose documents repeatedly delay clearance is not cheaper when shipments add demurrage and broker rework - assign each supplier a realistic friction cost from past orders.

## Building the Side-by-Side Comparison Matrix

With every quote normalized, the comparison becomes a spreadsheet exercise. A practical workflow:

1. Fix the basis: one specification, assay basis, Incoterm, currency, target quantity, and delivery schedule.
2. Convert prices: restate every unit price to cost per kilogram of PS content and to landed cost per kilogram delivered.
3. List inclusions: mark each quote against a fixed checklist - testing scope, document pack, packaging standard, split-delivery allowance.
4. Score non-price factors: lead time, document quality from past interactions, sample performance, responsiveness, and financial terms.
5. Present two views: the pure price ranking and the total-cost ranking; when they disagree, the gap is the most useful negotiation conversation.

| Criterion | Supplier A | Supplier B | Supplier C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Price per kg PS content (normalized) | - | - | - |
| Landed cost per kg delivered | - | - | - |
| COA scope matches your spec | - | - | - |
| Document pack included | - | - | - |
| Packaging standard (nitrogen flush, drum) | - | - | - |
| MOQ fit to annual demand | - | - | - |
| Lead time and safety-stock impact | - | - | - |
| Payment terms | - | - | - |

The matrix makes the decision defensible: every preference you express to management maps to a line the suppliers themselves quoted.

## FAQ

### Q: Why do phosphatidylserine quotes differ so much per kilogram?

Because the quotes describe different products. Source, assay, test and document inclusions, packaging, and Incoterm each move the number by double digits. Normalize to price per kilogram of PS content at a fixed Incoterm and the spread narrows sharply.

### Q: Should we simply choose the lowest normalized quote?

Price is one column, not the verdict. A low quote that excludes required testing, uses weaker packaging, or forces a large MOQ often carries the highest total cost - rank suppliers on landed cost plus non-price scoring.

### Q: How do we handle quotes in different currencies and validity windows?

Restate all quotes into your reporting currency using the same rate source and date, and record each validity window. If one price expires before your purchasing cycle ends, ask for a written extension rather than comparing an expiring price against a firm one.

### Q: Which costs are most often forgotten in a PS comparison?

Border re-testing, demurrage from document delays, safety stock from long lead times, split-delivery surcharges, and per-document charges for required statements.

## Conclusion

A phosphatidylserine quotation bundles product definition, quality scope, commercial terms, and logistics assumptions - and the cheapest-looking bundle is rarely the cheapest once unpacked. By fixing a single comparison basis, normalizing prices to PS content and landed cost, pricing the testing, packaging, and documentation you will actually need, and scoring delivery reliability alongside price, procurement teams move from comparing numbers to comparing offers - and carry a matrix that stands up to internal review.
