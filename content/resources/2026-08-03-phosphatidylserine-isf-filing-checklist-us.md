## Why ISF Is a Separate Import Task

### The 10+2 rule is advance cargo-security data, not an entry or FDA filing

The ISF rule, published as an interim final rule in 2008 and in force since January 26, 2009, requires importers and vessel operating carriers to provide advance electronic data on non-bulk cargo shipments arriving in the United States by vessel. CBP program materials describe the initiative as "10+2": ten importer data elements plus two carrier requirements, the vessel stow plan and container status messages.

For phosphatidylserine, the distinction matters because buyers routinely merge several obligations into one "import paperwork" bucket. The ISF answers a security question: what cargo is moving, who is behind it, and where it was stuffed. FDA prior notice answers a different question about food imports, FSVP assigns hazard-control responsibility to the U.S. importer, and the CBP entry determines duty, release, and liquidation while a customs bond secures those obligations.

### ISF ownership and who can transmit

Under 19 CFR 149.1, the ISF Importer is the party causing goods to arrive in a U.S. port by vessel, which for shipments other than foreign cargo remaining on board will be the goods' owner, purchaser, consignee, or an agent such as a licensed customs broker. An authorized agent may submit the filing, but the regulation requires the agent to retain powers of attorney in English.

What the buyer cannot delegate is the underlying data: seller identity, manufacturer identity, country of origin, HTS starting point, stuffing location, and consolidator all come from the transaction and the supplier. The [customs broker handoff checklist](/resources/phosphatidylserine-customs-broker-handoff-us/) explains how to keep the broker packet clean; the ISF is one specific filing inside that packet rather than the whole packet.

## The 24-Hour Filing Clock for Phosphatidylserine Shipments

### What must be transmitted at least 24 hours before vessel lading

Under 19 CFR 149.2(b), eight of the ten ISF elements are due no later than 24 hours before the cargo is laden aboard the vessel at the foreign port:

- seller
- buyer
- importer of record number
- consignee number(s)
- manufacturer (or supplier)
- ship to party
- country of origin
- commodity HTSUS number

For a drummed PS shipment, the practical deadline is set by the carrier and forwarder cut-off. "24 hours before lading" is the regulatory floor; in commercial practice, the forwarder will usually request the data earlier so it can validate the filing and resolve rejections. A buyer who waits for the final invoice to start collecting the manufacturer name, stuffing address, and HTS starting point is already compressing the window.

### The two elements tied to arrival, not lading

The remaining two importer elements, container stuffing location and consolidator (stuffer), are due as early as possible and in no event later than 24 hours prior to arrival in a U.S. port, or at lading for shorter voyages. These are usually the easiest fields for a PS supplier to confirm because they describe where the drums were stuffed and who arranged it. For a factory-load shipment, the factory address is the stuffing location and the manufacturer can be named as the consolidator.

### Updates, corrections, and the flexible elements

The ISF is not a one-time transmission. 19 CFR 149.2(d) requires the submitting party to update the filing if information changes or more accurate information becomes available before the goods enter a U.S. port, and to withdraw the filing if the goods are no longer intended for import. For the four flexible elements, CBP permits an initial response based on the best available data, with an update required as soon as more precise information is available and in no event less than 24 hours before arrival.

For PS importers, the workflow is simple: file on best-available data early, then update as the final invoice, packing list, bill of lading draft, and supplier confirmations arrive. The buyer should still send corrected manufacturer or stuffing details to the filer before arrival rather than assuming the first transmission is locked.

## A Practical ISF Data Checklist for PS Imports

Use this table to assign owners and collect the ten elements during order confirmation:

| ISF element | What CBP asks for | What the PS buyer should request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seller | Name and address of the entity selling the goods | Exact legal name and address from the quotation or sales contract, not a trading nickname |
| Buyer | Name and address of the entity buying the goods | The U.S. purchasing entity as shown on the purchase order |
| Importer of record number | IRS number, EIN, SSN, or CBP-assigned number | Confirm which U.S. entity holds importer of record responsibility |
| Consignee number(s) | IRS, EIN, SSN, or CBP-assigned number of the U.S. party on whose account goods ship | Match the consignee line on the bill of lading |
| Manufacturer (or supplier) | Entity that last manufactures, assembles, produces, or grows the commodity, or the supplier in the country of export | Legal company name and address, plus product route (general, soy, or sunflower PS) |
| Ship to party | First deliver-to party after customs release | The actual receiving facility, not only the corporate address |
| Country of origin | Country of manufacture, production, or growth | China for PS produced in China; confirm per shipment |
| Commodity HTSUS number | Duty/statistical reporting number, at least six digits | Confirm the HTS starting point under heading 2923 with the classification file |
| Container stuffing location | Physical location where goods were stuffed | Factory address if factory-loaded, or the consolidation warehouse |
| Consolidator (stuffer) | Party that stuffed the container or arranged stuffing | Manufacturer for factory-load, or the consolidator's legal name and address |

Three elements deserve extra attention: the manufacturer (or supplier), country of origin, and commodity HTSUS number must be linked at the line item level, so the buyer should send the supplier's legal identity, the route-specific product description, and the classification starting point together. ### Manufacturer (or supplier) identity

The ISF manufacturer element asks for the name and address of the entity that last manufactures the commodity, or the party supplying the finished goods in the country from which they leave. ### Container stuffing location and consolidator

For a drummed PS order, ask the supplier whether the shipment will be factory-loaded or consolidated. If the factory loads the container, the stuffing location is the factory address and the manufacturer is the consolidator. If a warehouse or logistics provider stuffs the container, capture that party's legal name and address.

The commodity HTSUS number must be provided at least to the six-digit level and is strongest when it matches the invoice and COA descriptions. ## Common Mistakes That Trigger ISF Problems

1. Treating ISF as an afterthought the broker will "just handle," without confirming who is the ISF Importer and who transmits.
2. Waiting for the final invoice to collect manufacturer identity, stuffing location, and the HTS starting point.
3. Using a brand name instead of the exact legal manufacturer identity in the supplier file.
4. Confusing ISF with prior notice, FSVP, the customs entry, or the customs bond, and assuming one filing covers the others.
5. Filing once and never updating the ISF when the bill of lading, stuffing address, or ship-to location changes.
6. Letting the commercial invoice describe the product differently from the COA, the classification file, and the ISF line item.

The practical fix is narrow and repeatable: **assign an ISF owner at order confirmation, collect the ten elements with the supplier, transmit on best-available data, and update before arrival.**

## FAQ

### Who files the ISF for a phosphatidylserine import into the United States?

The ISF Importer, defined under 19 CFR 149.1 as the party causing the goods to arrive in a U.S. port by vessel, must ensure the filing is transmitted. The owner, purchaser, or consignee usually arranges for a licensed customs broker or forwarder to transmit it as an authorized agent, while the importer remains responsible for accurate underlying data.

### Is ISF the same as prior notice, FSVP, or the customs entry?

No. ISF is an advance cargo-security filing required for non-bulk cargo arriving by vessel. FDA prior notice, FSVP, and the CBP entry are separate obligations that belong in the same shipment file but answer different questions.

### What ISF data should buyers request from a PS supplier before booking?

Buyers should request the exact legal manufacturer name and address, product route, country of origin, HTS starting point, container stuffing location, consolidator identity, and shipment quantities. Most can be confirmed at order confirmation, well before the 24-hour lading deadline.

### What happens if the ISF is filed late or contains wrong data?

CBP program materials describe liquidated damages of up to $5,000 per transmission with a $10,000 cap per ISF, and CBP may withhold release or transfer of the cargo until the required information is provided and reviewed. Transmit early and update accurately rather than finalizing at the last minute.

### Does a small PS sample shipped by ocean freight need an ISF?

If the sample is non-bulk cargo arriving by vessel, the ISF requirement applies regardless of the small quantity. Samples shipped by courier or air freight are not vessel cargo and do not trigger this rule. The [sample shipment checklist](/resources/phosphatidylserine-sample-shipment-checklist-us-eu/) explains how to keep sample and commercial shipment files separate.
